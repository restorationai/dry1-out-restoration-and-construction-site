## What Do You Do in the First 60 Seconds?

Shut off the water at the source, then kill the power to any area where water has reached outlets, switches, or the panel. If the burst is at a fixture (a washing machine hose, a water heater line, a bathroom supply valve), closing that fixture's own shutoff is often faster than finding the main. If you can't find either shutoff or the water is coming from inside a wall, close the home's main water valve, usually near the street-facing side of the house or in the garage, and leave the power off in any wet area until an electrician or your utility confirms it's safe.

Once the water is stopped, move anything you can lift off the floor, including rugs, electronics, and boxes, and open a door or window if the weather allows it to get some air moving. Don't run a wet/dry vac on standing water near an outlet, and don't step into standing water if you're not sure the power in that room is off.

## Who Do You Call First, a Plumber or a Restoration Company?

Call both, in roughly the same hour, but they do different jobs. A licensed plumber finds and repairs the broken pipe or fitting; a restoration company removes the water and dries the structure before mold or rot sets in. Neither one replaces the other.

The plumber's job is narrow and mechanical: locate the break, cut out and replace the damaged section of pipe, pressure-test the line, and confirm the repair holds. Until that repair happens, you don't know the leak is actually stopped, which is why this call usually goes out first or at the same time as the second one.

The restoration side starts the moment water hits your floors, walls, or subfloor, even while the plumber is still working. Standing water pulled from a Hayward home near the shoreline or from a Hayward Highlands two-story both need the same thing fast: extraction equipment, moisture mapping, and air movers and dehumidifiers placed before the water wicks further into drywall, baseboards, and flooring. ## What Happens in the First 24 Hours After You Call?

In the first 24 hours, the plumber isolates and repairs the source while the restoration crew extracts standing water and starts drying equipment running. A technician will walk the affected rooms with a moisture meter, photograph the damage for your claim file, and map which materials are wet enough to need removal versus dried in place.

Most clean, sudden pipe breaks start as what the industry calls Category 1 water, meaning it came from a sanitary supply line. That water doesn't stay clean once it's been sitting on a floor or inside a wall cavity for more than a few hours, which is why speed matters more than it might seem to from the living room. Drywall, baseboards, and carpet pad that stayed wet too long usually get cut out and replaced rather than dried. ## Will Homeowners Insurance Cover a Burst Pipe in Hayward?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage from a burst pipe, since it's an internal failure rather than gradual wear. According to the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance), this is one of the more commonly covered water losses, but coverage still depends on your specific policy language and whether the damage is tied to a maintenance issue the insurer can point to.

You also have a duty to mitigate further damage once the leak is found, meaning the insurer expects you to stop the water and start drying promptly rather than waiting. Photograph everything before cleanup starts, keep the plumber's invoice and the restoration company's documentation, and don't assume a repair is covered just because the pipe broke suddenly, gradual leaks and long-term seepage are typically excluded.
