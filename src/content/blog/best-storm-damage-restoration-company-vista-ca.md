## What should you look for in a storm damage restoration company?

Four things matter more than anything else: licensing, response speed, the ability to tarp or board up immediately, and whether the same company can finish the rebuild. A company missing any one of these usually means a second contractor, a second contract, and a second set of delays.

Vista storms tend to come in two flavors: winter atmospheric river systems that dump heavy, sustained rain and overwhelm roof flashing and gutters, and Santa Ana wind events that drive debris into siding and strip loose shingles. The flash flooding that hit San Diego County in January 2024 showed how fast standing water and wind damage can combine into a single claim with multiple damage types. A company set up for one but not the other leaves gaps.

## Is 24/7 emergency response actually available, or just advertised?

A real 24/7 storm damage company answers the phone and dispatches a crew any time, day or night, not just during business hours with an answering service. Dry 1 Out operates on a 24/7 schedule, so a call placed at 2 a.m. during an atmospheric river event reaches a live crew, not a voicemail.

A compromised roof section left exposed overnight during a sustained rain event can turn a localized leak into a full ceiling collapse by morning. Ask directly: "If I call at 11 p.m. tonight, who shows up?" The answer should be specific, not vague.

## Does the company handle mitigation and rebuild under one license, or will you need a second contractor?

The strongest storm damage companies can handle both mitigation and reconstruction under one contractor license, so the same crew that extracts water and tarps the roof also frames, drywalls, and finishes the rebuild. Dry 1 Out holds California contractor license #993442, searchable through the [CSLB's license lookup tool](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/OnlineServices/CheckLicenseII/CheckLicense.aspx), and offers both [reconstruction](/services/reconstruction/) and mitigation services under that one license.

Without this, a homeowner often ends up managing two separate vendors: one that dries the structure and leaves, and a second general contractor who has to be found, vetted, and scheduled separately for the rebuild. That gap is where projects stall for weeks. One license covering both means one point of contact, one schedule, and one company accountable for the finished result.

## Can they tarp or board up the property the same day?

Roof tarping and emergency board-up should happen within hours of the call, not days, because an open roof section or broken window keeps letting weather and intruders into the structure. [Emergency board-up](/services/emergency-board-up/) secures broken windows, doors, and compromised exterior openings, while roof tarping stops active water intrusion until permanent repairs can be scheduled.

Ask any company you're considering whether tarping and board-up are services they perform directly, with their own crew and materials on the truck, or whether they subcontract it out and you'll wait for a second company to arrive. Direct capability means faster securing of the property and less additional damage by the time drying equipment goes in.

## How do they handle the insurance side of a storm claim?

A company worth hiring documents the damage thoroughly from the first visit, because that documentation is what your insurance adjuster uses to approve the claim. That means dated photos, moisture readings, and a written scope of work before any demolition begins, consistent with the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) for water damage restoration.

Most homeowners insurance policies cover sudden wind and storm damage to the structure, such as a tree limb puncturing a roof or wind tearing off shingles, but coverage for flooding from rising water typically requires a separate flood policy, per the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance). Check your specific policy language, since coverage details vary by insurer.
