## Why Does In-House Reconstruction Matter After a Fire?

In-house reconstruction matters because it closes the gap between stabilizing your home and actually living in it again: one scope of work, one schedule, and no second company waiting on the first company's paperwork before they'll start framing.

Fire mitigation under the [ANSI/IICRC S700 Standard for Professional Fire and Smoke Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s700/) covers soot removal, structural cleaning, and odor control, but it stops short of rebuilding a torn-out wall or replacing scorched cabinetry. If that work gets subcontracted to a general contractor who wasn't on-site for the mitigation, you're explaining the damage history a second time, and the new crew is relying on someone else's notes about what got removed and why.

## How Do You Verify a Fire Restoration Company's License and Certifications?

You verify a contractor's license by running it through the [California CSLB license lookup tool](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/OnlineServices/CheckLicenseII/CheckLicense.aspx), and you verify IICRC training by asking the company for the technician's certificate number and cross-checking it against [IICRC's own site](https://www.iicrc.org/).

Don't take a logo or a truck wrap as proof. A CSLB license confirms the company is legally allowed to do rebuild work in California, not just mitigation. IICRC certification confirms the technicians running your job were trained to a documented standard, not just told to "wing it" on a water-soaked, soot-covered structure.
