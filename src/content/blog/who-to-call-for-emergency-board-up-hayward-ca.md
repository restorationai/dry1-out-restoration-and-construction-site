## When do you call 911 or the fire department instead of a board-up crew?

Call 911 first whenever there's an active fire, anyone trapped or injured, downed power lines, a gas smell, or a structure that looks unstable. Hayward Fire Department and PG&E need to clear the scene before any contractor, including a board-up team, can safely enter.

Once the fire department has released the property, or Hayward police have finished documenting a break-in, that's the point a restoration company's board-up crew can step in. For storm damage like a wind-broken window or a tree limb through a roof vent, with no fire or downed lines involved, you can call a board-up crew directly without waiting on a fire response.

If the opening is a sliding door or garage that was forced during a burglary, it's worth asking the responding officer for a case or report number before the crew boards it up. Some insurers and some landlords ask for that number alongside the board-up invoice.

## What happens when you call for a board-up?

Dry 1 Out's dispatcher asks what's open to the weather (a window, a door, a section of roof, a garage), whether police or fire have cleared the scene, and the property address, then sends a technician out with plywood, tarps, and fasteners sized to the opening. You can [request an emergency board-up](/services/emergency-board-up/) the same way, by phone, any hour.

Have this ready when you call: the address and any gate or access code, a quick description of what's open, whether a police report was filed for a break-in, and your insurance company's name if you have it handy. None of it is required to get a crew moving, but it speeds things up.

On arrival, the crew typically photographs the damage before touching anything (your insurer will want this), measures and cuts plywood to the exact opening, secures it with structural screws rather than finish nails, tarps any roof or skylight openings, and leaves you a written record of what was boarded and when. ## Should you call your insurance company before or after the board-up happens?

Call for the board-up first, then notify your insurer. Most homeowners policies make the policyholder responsible for limiting further damage after a loss, and a secured opening is part of that duty. Waiting on an adjuster before covering a broken window just lets rain, wind, or another break-in add damage that was preventable.

Keep the board-up invoice, the before photos, and any police report number together. Emergency mitigation expenses like board-up are typically reimbursable under a standard policy, though exact coverage depends on your specific policy language, so confirm with your agent. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/settling-insurance-claims-after-a-disaster) has a plain-language overview of how disaster claims get settled if you want to read ahead of the adjuster's call.

## When does a board-up turn into a full fire or smoke damage restoration job?

A board-up only secures the opening, it doesn't address soot on contents, water used to fight the fire, or smoke odor that's worked into drywall and insulation. Those need a separate scope of work.

If the opening came from a house fire, Dry 1 Out's Hayward crews carry IICRC FSRT (fire and smoke) training and follow the [ANSI/IICRC S700 standard](https://iicrc.org/s700/) for fire and smoke restoration once the structure is secure. That can mean [fire damage restoration](/services/fire-damage-restoration/) for charred structure and contents, [smoke damage restoration](/services/smoke-damage-restoration/) for odor and soot that spread beyond the fire's reach, or both. Our [fire damage restoration process](/blog/fire-damage-restoration-process/) post walks through what each phase actually involves, from the first walkthrough to the final odor check. The [U.S. Fire Administration](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/after-a-fire/) and the [American Red Cross](https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/types-of-emergencies/fire/home-fire-recovery.html) both have useful first-24-hours checklists for anyone dealing with a home fire for the first time.

A break-in or storm-damaged window usually stops at the board-up and a glass repair, unless water got into the wall cavity or insulation, in which case it's worth a moisture check before anyone closes the wall back up.
