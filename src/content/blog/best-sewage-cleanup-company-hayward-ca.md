## What should a sewage cleanup company in Hayward actually do first?

A qualified crew treats sewage backup as Category 3 water, also called black water, the moment they walk in, because any backup coming from beyond the trap (a floor drain, a toilet with feces present, a main line clog pushing back into the house) is grossly contaminated under the [ANSI/IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s500/). That classification determines everything else: which materials get removed instead of dried, what PPE the crew wears, and whether antimicrobial treatment is required before drying even starts.

Most homeowners don't know the category system, and that's fine, that's not their job. But it means you can ask one simple question on the phone: "Do you treat this as Category 3 and remove affected porous materials, or do you just extract and dry?" A company that hesitates on that question is telling you something.

Porous materials like carpet padding, drywall, and insulation that touched Category 3 water are typically removed, not salvaged. Hard surfaces get cleaned, disinfected, and verified dry before anything gets rebuilt.

## Why does it matter if cleanup and reconstruction come from one company?

It matters because sewage cleanup almost always leaves a hole behind, cut drywall, removed flooring, sometimes a section of subfloor, and whoever closes that hole back up needs a general contracting license, not just a restoration certification. If the cleanup crew and the rebuild crew are two separate companies, you're now managing two contracts, two schedules, and two companies pointing fingers if something doesn't line up.

## What questions should you ask before hiring a sewage cleanup company?

Ask for their IICRC Certified Firm status, their contractor license number, and a written scope of work before anyone signs anything. A legitimate company answers all three without hesitation and can show documentation, not just tell you verbally.

Specific questions worth asking on the first call:

- "Are you an IICRC Certified Firm, and which individual certifications do your techs hold?" (Water restoration techs should hold WRT; structural drying work should be led by someone with ASD.)
- "Will you give me a written scope before you start tearing anything out?"
- "Does my homeowners policy typically cover this, or do I need a separate endorsement?" (Standard policies often exclude sewer and sump backups unless you carry a water backup endorsement; a company that tells you "insurance always covers this" is not being straight with you.)
- "What's your contractor license number, and does it cover the rebuild work too?"
- "Who's my single point of contact from start to finish?"

For more on what a straight answer about coverage actually sounds like, see [Who to Call for a Sewage Backup in Hayward, CA](/blog/who-to-call-for-sewage-backup-hayward-ca/).

## What are red flags in a sewage cleanup quote?

The biggest red flag is a verbal "we'll figure out pricing as we go" with no written scope, because sewage jobs involve material removal decisions that should be documented, not improvised on-site. A second red flag: a quote that promises your insurance will "definitely" cover the full job before anyone's even looked at your policy.

Other things to watch for: a company that can't produce an IICRC certification number when asked twice, a crew that shows up without visible PPE for what should be a Category 3 job, or pricing that's dramatically below every other estimate you've gotten, restoration work priced far under market usually means corners are getting cut on material removal, drying time, or antimicrobial treatment.
