## When Is It a Plumber Call Instead of a Mold Call?

If water is actively leaking or flowing right now, call a plumber or shut off the water first, before anyone starts mold removal. Mold remediation on wet, still-leaking material is wasted work, since the moisture source has to be stopped before drying and cleanup can hold.

The same logic applies to a few other situations:

- **Active water source**: burst supply line, running toilet, failed water heater. Shut off the water at the valve, then call a plumber if you can't stop it yourself.
- **Electrical or gas hazard**: scorched outlets, a gas smell, water near a breaker panel. Call 911 or your utility first. Don't touch anything electrical in a wet area.
- **Mold with no active leak**: a damp crawlspace under a Hayward Highlands home, condensation on a bathroom ceiling, or mold that's been there a while with the source already fixed. This is a straight remediation call.
- **A mild surface spot smaller than roughly 10 square feet on a hard, non-porous surface** (like tile) may be something a homeowner can clean per [EPA guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home). Anything larger, anything on porous material like drywall or carpet, or anything you can smell but not see belongs to a professional.

If you're not sure which category you're in, that's a reasonable first question to ask on the phone. A company that's handled Bay Area housing stock, from older homes near the Hayward Japanese Gardens to newer builds toward the shoreline, can usually tell from a description whether it's urgent plumbing or a remediation job.

## Do You Need a Mold Inspection Before Remediation Starts?

A mold inspection is worth getting first when you can't see the mold directly but suspect it, such as behind a wall, under flooring, or after a past leak that was dried but never tested. If you can already see visible growth, a remediation company can typically assess it directly and skip a separate inspection step.

An inspection uses tools like a moisture meter, a borescope to look inside wall cavities, and sometimes air or surface sampling sent to a lab. That gives you a documented scope before any demolition starts, which matters if you're a landlord, a buyer in a pending sale, or filing an insurance claim. [Dry 1 Out's mold inspection and testing service](/services/mold-inspection-testing/) is a separate line item from remediation for exactly this reason: the inspection tells you what's there, the remediation removes it. For a deeper look at the testing options, see our guide on [how to test for mold](/blog/how-to-test-for-mold/).

If the mold followed a known event, like a pipe that burst in a Fairway Park kitchen last year, the path is usually faster: document the moisture history, assess the current growth, and move straight to remediation. Mold can begin growing on wet material within 24 to 48 hours, according to the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home), so growth after a known leak isn't unusual and doesn't necessarily require a separate testing phase before work starts. ## What Happens When You Call for Mold Removal in Hayward?

When you call, expect to describe the location, size, and likely cause of the mold, and to get guidance on whether it's an inspection or a direct remediation job. On the visit, a technician checks moisture levels with a meter, identifies the extent of the growth, and sets up containment, plastic sheeting and negative air pressure, before any material is disturbed. That keeps spores from spreading to the rest of the house during removal. Affected porous material (drywall, insulation, sometimes carpet) usually gets removed rather than cleaned, since mold grows into porous surfaces in a way that's hard to fully remove by wiping. The space is then treated with an antimicrobial and, where appropriate, cleared with a final check before containment comes down.

If insurance is part of the picture, ask what documentation the remediation company provides, photos, moisture readings, a written scope, since that paperwork is what your adjuster will want to see.

Mold connected to a sudden, covered water loss, like a burst pipe, is sometimes covered under a standard homeowners policy, though most policies cap mold coverage or require it to trace back to a covered event. A slow, long-term leak typically isn't covered.
