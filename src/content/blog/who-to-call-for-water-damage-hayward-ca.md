## Who should I call first for water damage in Hayward?

Call a water damage restoration company first whenever the water source has already been stopped, which is the most common scenario (a burst pipe behind a wall, a dishwasher line that failed overnight, a roof leak after the first rain). The reason restoration comes before insurance or cleanup crews you find online: a restoration company extracts standing water, sets drying equipment, and documents the loss with moisture readings and photos the same visit. That documentation becomes the backbone of your insurance claim, and the faster drying starts, the less demolition is needed later.

## When should I call a plumber instead?

Call a plumber first only when water is still actively running and the source can't be shut off at the main or a local valve. A restoration crew can extract water and set up drying, but they don't fix a split pipe or a failed water heater, that's plumbing work.

In practice, most Hayward calls need both: a plumber stops the leak (or you find the shutoff yourself, usually a valve near the water meter or under the fixture), then a restoration company handles everything the water left behind. If you can stop the flow yourself, do that first, then call restoration, since every extra minute of standing water adds to the drying timeline.

## When is this a 911 or utility call, not a restoration call?

Call 911 or your utility first whenever there's an active safety hazard: standing water near an electrical panel or outlets, a gas smell, or structural sagging in a ceiling that looks ready to collapse. Those situations need the power or gas shut off before anyone, including a restoration crew, can safely work in the space.

Once the hazard is controlled, water damage restoration picks up from there. Don't try to shut off a breaker while standing in water, and don't re-enter a room with a bulging, water-logged ceiling until it's been checked.

## What about calling my insurance company first?

Call your insurance company after mitigation has started, not before. Most homeowners policies require you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, so stopping the water and starting extraction first actually protects your claim rather than delaying it.

A restoration company that documents the loss properly, photos, moisture maps, a written scope, gives your adjuster what they need without you having to explain the damage yourself. For a broader look at what's typically covered (a sudden burst pipe usually is, a slow leak you ignored for months usually isn't) see [our explainer on homeowners insurance and water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/). If the water came from a backed-up drain or sewer line, that's Category 3 black water, and most standard policies only cover it with a separate water backup endorsement, worth checking before you assume it's included.

The first call is a short triage conversation: what's flooded, how long it's been wet, and whether the source is stopped. Have your policy number handy if you have it, but don't wait on insurance to make the call.
