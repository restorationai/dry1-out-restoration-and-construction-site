Spray paint across a garage door, a shattered storefront window, kicked-in drywall, or a tenant's unit trashed after an eviction all land in the same category of damage: deliberate, often sudden, and frequently tied to a police report you'll need for your insurance file. Vandalism damage rarely shows up alone. Broken glass means exposed interiors. Forced entry means compromised locks and door frames. Graffiti on porous surfaces like stucco or unsealed brick sets within hours, and the longer it sits, the harder it is to lift without damaging the substrate underneath.

## What Vandalism Damage Cleanup and Repair actually involves

This work splits into two phases that often overlap: cleanup and repair. Cleanup covers glass and debris removal, graffiti stripping, paint or solvent residue extraction from flooring and fixtures, and disposal of anything broken beyond salvage. Repair covers the physical damage left behind: broken windows, pried door frames, punched drywall, torn screens, damaged siding, and in commercial spaces, gutted signage or fixtures. Equipment ranges from pressure washers and chemical graffiti removers rated for specific surfaces (masonry, metal, glass, painted wood) to standard framing and drywall tools for structural repair. Timeline depends heavily on scope: a single tagged wall might be cleaned and repainted in a day, while a break-in with forced entry, broken fixtures, and interior damage can run a week or more once materials are ordered and repairs are scheduled.

If the scene involves bodily fluids, needles, or other biological material, that portion of the cleanup falls under biohazard protocols rather than standard vandalism cleanup, and we handle that separately with the appropriate containment.

## Our process

1. **Documentation first.** Before anything is touched, we photograph and log every affected surface and item. This record supports your police report and becomes the backbone of your insurance claim.
2. **Secure the property.** Broken windows, pried doors, or compromised entry points get boarded or temporarily secured so the property isn't exposed to weather or a second break-in while repairs are scoped.
3. **Debris and residue removal.** Glass, paint cans, broken fixtures, and loose debris are cleared. Surface-specific graffiti removers are matched to the substrate so we're not stripping paint off stucco with a method meant for sealed metal.
4. **Surface and structural repair.** Drywall, trim, door frames, window glazing, and siding are repaired or replaced to match existing materials as closely as possible.
5. **Final walkthrough and scope confirmation.** We verify the space matches the written scope before calling the job complete, and flag anything that needs a specialty trade (electrical, glass fabrication) we don't handle directly.

## What separates a good vandalism response from a bad one

A rushed cleanup often skips proper surface testing before applying graffiti remover, which can etch stucco, bleach unsealed wood, or strip factory finish off metal doors, turning a cosmetic problem into a replacement job. Adjusters also look closely at whether forced-entry damage (splintered frames, broken strike plates) was documented before repair, since that's often the detail that separates a vandalism claim from a general liability dispute. Good operators photograph damage at multiple angles, keep a written scope before starting work, and don't paint over graffiti without sealing the surface first, since paint alone usually lets the pigment bleed back through within weeks.
