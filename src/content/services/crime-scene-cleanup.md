## What Crime Scene Cleanup actually involves

Crime scene cleanup, sometimes called forensic cleanup or post-crime cleanup, covers the removal and disposal of blood, bodily fluids, and other biological material from a residence, vehicle, or commercial space following a homicide, suicide, unattended death, or violent injury. The work goes beyond surface cleaning. Porous materials such as carpet, subflooring, drywall, and upholstery that have absorbed biological fluid typically cannot be fully disinfected and are removed and disposed of as regulated waste. Nonporous surfaces, including hard flooring, tile, and sealed wood, are cleaned with EPA-registered disinfectants and hospital-grade enzymatic solutions designed to break down protein-based residue. A technician trained in bloodborne pathogen handling works the scene in full PPE, following chain-of-custody documentation for any waste transported offsite. Depending on scope, a single-room cleanup runs a few hours; a scene involving structural removal or extended decomposition can take one to several days.

## Our process

1. **Scene coordination and scope.** We confirm the scene has been officially released, document existing conditions with photographs, and build a written scope describing what will be removed, cleaned, and disposed of.
2. **PPE and containment staging.** Technicians suit up in protective equipment rated for bloodborne pathogen exposure and establish containment barriers to prevent cross-contamination into unaffected areas of the home.
3. **Removal of affected materials.** Porous materials that have absorbed biological fluid, flooring, drywall, insulation, are removed and bagged as regulated medical waste according to state transport requirements.
4. **Enzymatic treatment and disinfection.** Remaining nonporous surfaces are treated with enzymatic cleaners that break down residual protein, followed by hospital-grade disinfection of the full affected area.
5. **Deodorization and clearance check.** Air scrubbing and odor neutralization address any lingering odor from biological breakdown, and a final visual and instrument check confirms the space meets clearance standards before we hand it back.
6. **Waste manifest and closeout documentation.** A regulated waste manifest and full photo record are provided for the family, property manager, and insurance carrier.

## What separates a good crime scene cleanup response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating a scene like a standard deep clean, scrubbing visible surfaces while leaving biological material trapped under flooring, inside wall cavities, or in subfloor seams, where it continues to pose a health hazard and produce odor weeks later. A thorough operator tests beyond the visible stain, checks for fluid migration into adjoining rooms, and knows when a porous material has to come out rather than be cleaned. Waste handling is another point adjusters and health departments scrutinize closely: biological waste has to be bagged, labeled, and transported by someone following the state's regulated medical waste protocols, not disposed of in a standard dumpster. Documentation matters just as much as the cleaning itself. Insurance carriers and, in some cases, law enforcement want a clear photographic and written record of what was found, what was removed, and how disposal was handled, which is why we build that record into every job from the first walkthrough.
