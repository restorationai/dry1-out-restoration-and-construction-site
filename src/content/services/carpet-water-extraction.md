**Carpet soaked after a pipe burst, water heater failure, or storm backup in Vista?** We answer 24/7. Carpet holds water the way a sponge does: the surface can feel damp while the pad underneath stays fully saturated for days, feeding mold growth and a sour, musty odor that gets worse the longer it sits. Walking across wet carpet and hearing it squelch, or noticing a cold, heavy spot that won't dry on its own, means the pad and possibly the subfloor are already holding standing moisture that needs mechanical extraction, not a fan and an open window.

## What Carpet Water Extraction actually involves

Carpet water extraction is the targeted removal of water from carpet fiber, carpet pad, and the flooring beneath it, using extraction equipment strong enough to pull moisture out of both the face yarn and the pad backing. It's a different problem than drying a hard-surface floor: carpet pad acts like a reservoir, and ordinary extraction tools skim the surface without touching what's trapped below. Depending on how much water intruded and how long it sat, the pad may be salvageable and dried in place, or it may need to be cut and removed so the subfloor and tack strips can dry properly. Either way, the goal in the first hours on site is pulling free water out fast, because carpet pad left wet past 24 to 48 hours starts to break down and becomes a mold risk.

## Our process

1. **Moisture mapping and water categorization.** We check the carpet, pad, and subfloor with moisture meters and classify the water (clean, gray, or contaminated) per IICRC S500 guidelines, since that determines whether the pad can be dried or has to come out.
2. **Containment and furniture staging.** Furniture gets blocked up on foil or blocks to prevent wicking and tannin staining, and we isolate the wet area so drying equipment and airflow stay concentrated where it's needed.
3. **Extraction.** We run weighted or deep extraction tools that pull water from the pad through the carpet face, not just surface water, using truck-mounted or high-capacity portable extractors depending on access and volume.
4. **Pad decision and subfloor check.** Pad that's been soaked by clean water can sometimes be dried in place with air movers underneath the carpet; pad exposed to contaminated water or saturated too long gets cut and removed, and we check the subfloor for cupping or delamination before resetting carpet.
5. **Drying verification.** Air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture readings in the pad, carpet, and subfloor match dry industry benchmarks, not until the carpet simply feels dry to the touch.

## What separates a good Carpet Water Extraction response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating carpet like a hard floor: running a wet-vac over the surface, setting out a couple of fans, and calling it done. That leaves the pad wet, and wet pad under dry-feeling carpet is exactly the kind of hidden moisture that turns into a mold claim three weeks later. A thorough response pulls back the carpet to inspect and extract the pad directly rather than extracting through the carpet alone, and checks moisture at the tack strip and baseboard, where water tends to collect and get missed.

Adjusters reviewing a carpet water loss generally want to see daily moisture logs, photos of pad condition before any removal, and documentation of the water category, since that affects whether pad and carpet are restorable or need replacement. A response that skips documentation, or that dries carpet in place when the pad was contaminated, tends to generate callbacks and disputed claims later.
