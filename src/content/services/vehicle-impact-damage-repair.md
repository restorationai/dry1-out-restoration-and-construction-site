A car doesn't have to be going fast to take out a stud wall, a garage header, or a gas meter. Vehicle impact damage ranges from a bent mailbox and cracked siding to a vehicle sitting halfway inside a living room, and the two look nothing alike once you get past the drywall. The opening left behind is often structural, not cosmetic, and it needs to be stabilized before anyone decides what repair actually looks like.

## What Vehicle Impact Damage Repair actually involves

This work sits at the intersection of structural carpentry and property restoration. A vehicle strike can shear through siding and sheathing into framing, crack or displace foundation block, sever electrical runs, or compromise a load-bearing header in a garage or carport. The visible hole is rarely the whole story: a bumper impact that looks contained to one wall section can rack an adjoining wall out of plumb or crack a slab footing several feet away. Work typically starts with stabilization (temporary shoring, bracing, or board-up) within hours of the call, followed by a structural walkthrough, utility checks, and then framing, sheathing, and finish repair sized to match the original construction. Timelines vary widely: a damaged porch post and siding panel might close out in days, while a garage wall with a severed header and bent rim joist can take weeks once an engineer and permitting are involved.

## Our process

1. **Stabilization and temporary shoring.** Before anything else, we brace or shore any wall, header, or column that's carrying load it was never designed to carry mid-damage, so the structure doesn't shift further or become unsafe to work around.
2. **Structural assessment.** We trace the impact path through the wall assembly, not just the surface, checking studs, headers, sill plates, and foundation connections for cracking, splitting, or displacement that isn't visible from inside the room.
3. **Utility and safety check.** Vehicle strikes frequently clip electrical panels, gas lines, water lines, or meter bases. We identify anything that needs to be shut down or inspected by the utility or a licensed trade before work proceeds.
4. **Board-up and weather protection.** The opening gets sealed against weather, pests, and unauthorized entry while the repair scope is finalized, using our emergency board-up service as the bridge between impact and reconstruction.
5. **Structural repair and finish matching.** Framing, sheathing, siding, and interior finishes are rebuilt to match the surrounding construction, with our reconstruction team handling anything beyond simple patch repair.

## What separates a good vehicle impact response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating the damage as cosmetic because the hole looks small from the driveway. A header that's been struck can lose load capacity without visibly cracking, and a foundation wall can hairline-crack in a way that only shows up months later as a sticking door or sloped floor. Adjusters look for documented structural assessment, not just "patched and painted" photos, especially when the strike involved a garage, carport, or any area with a load-bearing wall. Good operators check square and plumb on adjoining walls, not just the impact zone, and loop in a structural engineer when framing members are compromised rather than guessing at a repair. They also document pre-existing damage versus impact damage clearly, since insurers on both the homeowner and at-fault driver's side will ask for that distinction.

## What does Vehicle Impact Damage Repair cost?

Costs vary enormously depending on whether the impact hit a fence, a garage door, or a load-bearing wall, and whether structural framing or foundation work is involved. These are typical industry ranges, not a quote: every loss gets its own written scope before work begins, since no two impacts damage the same combination of framing, siding, and utilities.

| Scenario | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Fence, mailbox, or minor siding damage | $400 - $1,800 |
| Garage door and frame replacement | $1,500 - $5,000 |
| Porch column or non-load-bearing wall section | $2,500 - $7,500 |
| Load-bearing wall or header with framing repair | $7,000 - $20,000 |
| Foundation or slab damage requiring engineer review | $10,000 - $35,000+ |

Homeowners insurance typically covers vehicle impact to a structure under the dwelling portion of the policy, and the at-fault driver's auto liability coverage may also apply depending on the circumstances; which policy pays, and how much, usually gets sorted out between the carriers once documentation is in hand.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

In Vista, impact damage calls tend to cluster around driveways, carports, and attached garages, which is where most vehicle-to-structure impacts happen in residential neighborhoods. Dry, sunny stretches mean board-up and temporary protection usually isn't racing against rain, but any opening left exposed still invites pests and dust intrusion, so sealing it quickly still matters even without a storm on the way.
