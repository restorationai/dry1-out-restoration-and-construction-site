A dishwasher that's been seeping for three weeks looks nothing like a washing machine supply line that let go overnight. One soaks slowly into particleboard cabinet floors until the toe kick swells and the laminate starts to lift. Appliance leak cleanup covers both ends of that spectrum, and the right response depends on catching which one you're actually dealing with.

## What Appliance Leak Cleanup actually involves

Appliance leaks are deceptive because the visible mess rarely matches the hidden damage. A refrigerator leak cleanup often starts with a damp spot on the kitchen floor, but the water has usually already tracked under the cabinet toe kick and along the subfloor seam toward the dishwasher bay. A washing machine flood from a burst hose can saturate the subfloor, the wall cavity behind the unit, and the ceiling of the room below if the laundry sits on a second floor, which is common in Vista's newer two-story builds.

We start by locating and isolating the source, whether that's a supply line, a drain hose, an ice maker line, or a water heater fitting that's been slowly weeping into a pan. Water heater leak cleanup in particular needs a close look at the surrounding framing and insulation, since tank failures often go unnoticed in garages and closets for days before anyone spots the puddle. From there, extraction equipment pulls standing water, and moisture meters map how far it traveled under flooring, behind cabinetry, and inside walls. Drying equipment goes in once we know the real extent, not just the visible puddle.

## Our process

1. **Source identification and shutoff.** We confirm which line, hose, valve, or seal failed and make sure the water is fully stopped before anything else happens.
2. **Extraction and appliance removal.** Standing water is pulled from flooring, and the appliance is pulled out or disconnected as needed to access the cavity behind and beneath it.
3. **Moisture mapping.** Meters and sometimes thermal imaging trace how far the water migrated under cabinets, subfloor, and into adjacent wall cavities, which is where most of the hidden damage actually lives.
4. **Targeted drying.** Air movers and dehumidifiers go exactly where the moisture mapping says they're needed, not just where the leak was visible, with drying documented per the IICRC S500 standard.
5. **Moisture verification before closeout.** We recheck affected materials before cabinets, flooring, or drywall go back together, so nothing gets closed up still wet.

## What separates a good appliance leak response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating the visible puddle as the whole job. A dishwasher leak cleanup that stops at mopping the kitchen floor misses the particleboard cabinet base, which can stay saturated and swollen long after the surface looks dry. An ice maker line leak behind a refrigerator is often a slow drip that's been running for weeks, which means mold risk has to be evaluated, not assumed away just because the water is gone.

Adjusters reviewing appliance leak claims want to see documented moisture readings at the start and end of drying, photos of the source and the affected cavity, and a clear explanation of how far the water traveled. A response that skips moisture mapping and just dries the carpet or floor surface tends to get questioned later if damage resurfaces, and it leaves the homeowner exposed if mold shows up in a wall cavity that was never checked.
