## What Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning actually involves

Framers leave sawdust in wall cavities. Drywall crews leave a film of fine gypsum dust on every horizontal surface, inside cabinet boxes, and coating light fixtures. Painters leave overspray on windows and hardware. None of that shows up on a punch list, but it's the difference between a space that looks finished and one that's actually ready to live or work in. Post-construction cleaning is a distinct trade from routine housekeeping: it's dust extraction from HVAC returns and registers, adhesive and paint overspray removal from glass and flooring, interior window and track cleaning, cabinet interior wipe-down, and a final detail pass that catches what the construction crew's broom missed. On a full remodel or new build, this typically runs in phases: a rough clean after demo and framing, then a final clean once trim, paint, and flooring are complete.

## Our process

1. **Walkthrough and scope** - we go room by room with the general contractor or homeowner to identify dust load, residue type (paint, adhesive, grout haze, joint compound), and any surfaces needing special handling, like natural stone or pre-finished hardwood.
2. **Dust extraction** - HEPA vacuuming of wall cavities, baseboards, window tracks, light fixtures, and HVAC supply and return vents before any wet cleaning starts, so dust doesn't turn into paste once it's wet.
3. **Surface detail cleaning** - interior and exterior glass, cabinet interiors and exteriors, countertops, fixtures, and hardware, with product matched to the surface so you don't strip a sealed finish or haze a stone counter.
4. **Floor-specific finishing** - tile and grout haze removal, hardwood dust and polish pass, or carpet vacuuming and edge detailing, depending on what's underfoot.
5. **Final walkthrough** - a last pass against the punch list, flagging anything that needs the GC's attention (paint touch-up, a sticking door) before the space is handed back over.

## What separates a good post-construction cleaning response from a bad one

The most common miss is cleaning wet before the dust is pulled out dry. Wipe a dusty surface with a damp rag and you get a smear instead of a clean finish, then someone has to go back over it twice. A second common problem is using the wrong product on a new finish: all-purpose cleaner on honed stone, ammonia-based glass cleaner on tinted low-E windows, or an abrasive pad on brushed hardware, all of which can mark a surface that was just installed and is still under warranty. Good crews sequence the work (dust first, top to bottom, dry to wet) and know which materials in the job need a gentler touch. If the project is tied to an insurance claim (fire or water repair followed by reconstruction), adjusters will also want to see that the final clean is documented as part of the completed scope, not billed as a separate undocumented add-on.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Vista's dry, dusty summer and fall months mean a construction site accumulates more airborne dust between phases, and Santa Ana wind events can push outdoor dust and ash back into a space right after a final clean if windows and doors aren't sealed at the right stage. Scheduling the final clean as close to project handoff as possible, rather than days ahead of it, keeps the finished space from needing a repeat pass.
