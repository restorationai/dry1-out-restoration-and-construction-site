## Restoration emergencies common in Castro Valley

Castro Valley sits in a bowl at the base of the East Bay hills, and that geography shapes the damage we see. Winter storms push runoff down from the surrounding ridgelines into neighborhoods with limited storm drain capacity, which means basements and below-grade additions can take on water even when the rain itself isn't extreme. The housing stock skews older, with many homes built in the mid-20th century on hillside lots, so aging cast iron drain lines, galvanized supply pipes, and roofs well past their service life all factor into why pipe failures and attic leaks are a regular call here. Summer and fall bring a different risk: dry hillside vegetation and periodic regional wildfire smoke that settles into HVAC systems and soft materials, which is why smoke and odor remediation requests tend to spike in late summer.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Castro Valley

Much of Castro Valley was built out from the 1950s through the 1970s, with wood-frame ranch and split-level homes on slab or raised foundations, plus a meaningful share of crawlspace construction on the hillside lots. Plumbing from that era is a mix of galvanized steel and copper, and homes untouched by renovation may still carry original cast iron drain lines, both of which are prone to corrosion-driven leaks decades on. Older homes can also carry asbestos-containing materials in flooring, siding, or duct wrap, which affects how a restoration scope is planned.

Castro Valley is unincorporated and sits close to the Hayward Fault, so structural repairs and rebuilds are permitted through Alameda County rather than a city building department, and seismic considerations factor into any framing or foundation work. Hillside soil in the area tends to hold water and shift seasonally, which is part of why drainage problems around foundations and crawlspaces recur in older homes built before modern grading standards.
