## Restoration emergencies common in San Lorenzo

San Lorenzo sits in the East Bay flatlands between San Leandro and Hayward, close enough to the Bay that winter storms arrive wet and persistent rather than brief. Heavy rain events push water against older foundations and through aging roof assemblies, and the clay-heavy soils common across this part of the East Bay drain slowly, which means standing water around a foundation can sit for days instead of hours. Add in a housing stock built largely in a single postwar wave, and the plumbing failures that come with 70-plus-year-old pipe runs, and you get a steady pattern of slab leaks, slow bathroom and kitchen leaks behind walls, and sewer line backups rather than dramatic flash floods. Fire risk here tends to track back to older electrical panels and aging appliances more than wildfire exposure, since San Lorenzo is urbanized and not adjacent to open wildland.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in San Lorenzo

Most of San Lorenzo was built in a concentrated postwar wave in the late 1940s and 1950s as one of the Bay Area's early planned suburban communities, which means a large share of homes share the same construction era: wood-frame walls, composition shingle roofs, and slab-on-grade or shallow crawlspace foundations. That uniform vintage also means galvanized supply lines, older cast iron drain runs, and in some homes original knob-and-tube wiring are still common, all of which factor into how a restoration crew plans a drying or repair scope. San Lorenzo is unincorporated, so structural repair and rebuild permits go through Alameda County rather than a city building department, which matters when a loss requires reconstruction beyond simple drywall and flooring replacement. The flat, low-lying terrain and clay-heavy soils typical of this part of the East Bay also mean water sitting against a foundation drains slowly, something we account for when assessing moisture intrusion at the slab or crawlspace level.
