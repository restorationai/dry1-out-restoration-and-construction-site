## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in San Francisco

Much of San Francisco's housing dates to the early 1900s rebuild after the 1906 earthquake and fire, with Victorian and Edwardian wood-frame construction dominating neighborhoods like Noe Valley and the Mission. These buildings typically sit on raised foundations or shallow basements rather than slabs, so water intrusion tends to travel along sill plates and floor joists before it's visible indoors. Plumbing in this era often mixes galvanized supply lines and cast iron drains with later copper or PEX repairs, and older buildings may still contain lead paint or asbestos-containing materials that affect how a restoration crew can open walls or floors.

The city sits on a mix of bedrock, sand, and fill, particularly in low-lying and reclaimed areas, which affects drainage and foundation movement differently block by block.
