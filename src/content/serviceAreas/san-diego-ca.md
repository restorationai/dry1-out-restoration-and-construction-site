## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in San Diego

Much of San Diego's housing was built from the 1940s through the 1970s, with stucco-over-wood-frame construction, slab-on-grade foundations in flatter neighborhoods, and raised foundations with crawlspaces more common in older hillside areas like North Park and Hillcrest. Slab construction means a slow leak often travels laterally before it surfaces, while crawlspace homes can trap moisture against floor joists for weeks before anyone notices. Plumbing vintage varies block by block: expect galvanized or cast iron in pre-1960s homes, copper through the 70s and 80s, and PEX in anything newer, each with different failure points a restoration scope has to account for.

San Diego's clay-heavy soils in inland valleys expand and contract with seasonal moisture, which stresses foundations and slab joints over time, while sandier coastal soils near the water drain faster but invite salt-laden moisture into crawlspaces.
