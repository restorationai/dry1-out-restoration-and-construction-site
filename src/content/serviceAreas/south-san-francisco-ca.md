## Restoration emergencies common in South San Francisco

South San Francisco sits on the San Francisco Peninsula, where marine fog, winter atmospheric river storms, and proximity to the bay shape the kinds of losses property owners deal with. Older roofs and siding take on moisture during the wet season, and homes built into the city's hillside terrain can see water intrusion at retaining walls and below-grade areas when storm drains surcharge. Flat-lying parcels closer to the bay sit on soft fill and historic bay mud, which drains slowly and keeps crawlspaces and foundations damp longer after a leak or storm than homes on firmer ground farther from the shoreline. That combination of persistent moisture and older building stock is also why mold calls often follow water events here by only a few days rather than weeks.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in South San Francisco

Much of South San Francisco's housing dates to the mid-20th century, with wood-frame construction, stucco or wood siding, and a mix of slab-on-grade and raised foundations with crawlspaces. Homes from that era commonly carry galvanized steel or early copper supply lines and cast iron drain lines, both of which corrode internally and fail without warning, and older finishes may contain asbestos or lead paint that a restoration scope has to plan around before demolition begins. Low-lying parcels near the bay sit on fill and soft bay mud, which holds moisture against foundations and slows drying after a leak or flood compared to the firmer soils on the city's hillsides. Structural repairs, rebuilds, and any work that opens walls or foundations require a permit through the City of South San Francisco's building division, and the Bay Area's seismic activity means older unreinforced elements are a common finding once a wall is opened for repair.
