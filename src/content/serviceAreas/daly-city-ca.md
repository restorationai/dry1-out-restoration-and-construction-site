## Restoration emergencies common in Daly City

Daly City's weather is defined by persistent coastal fog and onshore moisture rolling off the Pacific, which keeps humidity high year-round and slows natural drying after any water event. Combine that with a housing stock largely built during the postwar boom of the 1940s and 1950s, when San Francisco's population pushed south across the city line, and you get tightly built homes with aging plumbing, limited attic ventilation, and wall cavities that hold moisture longer than newer construction. Hillside lots common throughout the city also mean drainage and grading issues: water that doesn't shed properly off a roof or foundation tends to find its way into crawlspaces and lower-level rooms. Winter atmospheric river storms add wind-driven rain and occasional roof and gutter failures, while the fog itself contributes to slow, chronic moisture problems that can turn into mold if they go unaddressed.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Daly City

Much of Daly City's housing dates to the 1940s and 1950s boom years, built as tightly spaced single-family homes and duplexes, often slab-on-grade or with shallow crawlspaces on the city's hillside lots.
