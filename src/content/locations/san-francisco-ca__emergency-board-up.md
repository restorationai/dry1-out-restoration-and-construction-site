## Why San Francisco Properties See Emergency Board-Up Needs

San Francisco's building stock skews old, and a lot of it still carries original single-pane wood-sash windows, especially in the Victorian and Edwardian rowhouses that line streets in Noe Valley and Bernal Heights. Those frames crack and shatter differently than modern vinyl or aluminum units, and once a pane is gone the wood sash itself is exposed to the city's near-constant marine layer. Storefronts in SoMa and the Mission District see a different pattern: forced entry and vandalism leave plate glass in large, sharp panels that need fast, secure coverage, not just a sheet of plywood tacked over the frame. Wind off the Pacific and the fog that rolls through the Sunset and Richmond Districts most evenings also means an open window or door left unsecured for even a day can let in enough moisture to start softening wood trim and subfloor, on top of whatever caused the opening in the first place.

## Our Emergency Board-Up Process in San Francisco

We start by assessing the opening, broken glass, a kicked-in door, storm-torn siding, or a vehicle impact, and measuring for a board that actually seats flush against the original frame rather than a generic plywood cutout. Older Edwardian and Victorian window openings in neighborhoods like Pacific Heights and Noe Valley are rarely standard dimensions, so field measurement matters more here than in newer construction. We clear loose glass and debris, treat any splintered or water-softened wood framing so the board seals properly, and fasten weather-resistant plywood sized to the actual opening, not just the visible gap. For commercial storefronts near the Ferry Building or along busy SoMa corridors, we also factor in street-level visibility and foot traffic when choosing fastening methods, since a board that looks half-secured invites more trouble than one that's clearly locked down.

## San Francisco Permits, HOA & Historic District Considerations

A fair number of San Francisco neighborhoods, including parts of Noe Valley and Pacific Heights, fall under local historic preservation review, which can affect how a damaged exterior opening gets permanently repaired later, even though a temporary board-up itself is an emergency measure and not a permitted alteration. We document the condition of the original frame and trim with photos before boarding so your contractor or the Planning Department has a clear record of what was there before the damage, which matters more in a designated historic district than it would in a newer building. If the property is part of an HOA, particularly in condo conversions common in SoMa and parts of the Mission, we're glad to coordinate the board-up timing with building management so it doesn't conflict with any association notice requirements for exterior work.

## Local note

Fog-belt dampness in the Sunset and Richmond Districts does something specific to a board-up: the plywood itself can swell slightly within the first day or two if it isn't sealed on the exposed face, which loosens the fastener seat faster than it would in a drier part of the city. We seal the exterior face of the board on jobs out that way as a matter of course, since a board that's still tight a week later saves a callback and keeps the opening genuinely secure, not just temporarily covered.
