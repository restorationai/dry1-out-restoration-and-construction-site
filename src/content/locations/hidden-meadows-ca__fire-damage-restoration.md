## Why Hidden Meadows Properties See Fire Damage

Hidden Meadows sits in the wildland-urban interface east of Escondido, where chaparral-covered hillsides meet single-family lots on larger acreage. That terrain means two different fire exposures: direct wildfire impact during Santa Ana wind events in fall, and structure fires that start inside the home (electrical, kitchen, dryer vents) and spread quickly through dry attic framing during the region's long dry season. Many homes here were built in the 1970s and 80s with wood shake or older composition roofing later replaced, plus original electrical panels that weren't sized for today's appliance load. When fire touches those older systems, smoke and soot travel through HVAC ducting and wall cavities well beyond the room of origin, which is why a contained kitchen fire can still leave odor and residue in bedrooms on the opposite end of the house.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Hidden Meadows

We start with a structural safety assessment before anyone else moves through the property, since fire-damaged framing and compromised roof decking are common in the rural lots and detached garages typical of this area. From there, we board up or tarp openings to stop weather and wildlife intrusion (not uncommon given the surrounding open space), then move into content triage, soot and residue removal from hard surfaces, and HVAC duct cleaning to stop smoke odor from recirculating. Drying and deodorization follow the IICRC S700 fire restoration standard, with thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment chosen based on how deeply the smoke has penetrated porous materials like insulation and subflooring. On properties with septic systems, which many Hidden Meadows homes still use, we coordinate access carefully so heavy equipment and debris staging don't disturb leach field lines.
