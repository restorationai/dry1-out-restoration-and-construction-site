San Francisco's housing stock runs the gamut from Edwardian flats stacked shoulder-to-shoulder in the Mission District to mid-century stucco boxes out in the Sunset, and a fire, storm, or major water loss in any of them creates a rebuild problem that looks nothing like a suburban tract-home job. Reconstruction Services from Dry1 Out Restoration and Construction picks up where mitigation ends, framing, drywall, flooring, and finish work brought back to a condition that passes inspection and matches what was there before the damage.

## Why San Francisco Properties See Reconstruction Issues

A lot of the damage we rebuild after traces back to the city's building stock and its geography. Victorians and Edwardians in Noe Valley and Pacific Heights were built close together with shared walls and tight setbacks, which means a kitchen fire or roof leak in one unit often concentrates heat, smoke, or water into framing that's harder to access than it would be on a detached home. Out toward the Sunset and Richmond Districts, the fog belt keeps humidity high for months at a time, and once a roof leak or pipe failure gets into the wall cavity, the framing in these older homes holds moisture longer than it would in a drier part of the state. We also see a fair number of structural rebuilds tied to storm-driven water intrusion around flat and low-slope roofs, a common detail on the city's rowhouses.

## Our Reconstruction Services Process in San Francisco

We start with a structural assessment that documents what mitigation crews removed, whether that's fire-damaged joists, water-saturated subfloor, or plaster that had to come out to chase moisture. From there we scope the rebuild against San Francisco's permitting requirements, since even a straightforward drywall and framing repair in a pre-1950 building can trigger a Department of Building Inspection review if load-bearing elements were affected. Our crews work to match existing materials where it matters, plaster skim coats in a Victorian parlor read very differently than drywall mud, and a mismatched repair tends to get flagged by both inspectors and homeowners. Once framing, electrical, and rough work are signed off, we move to finish carpentry, paint, and flooring, closing the loop so the space is livable again rather than just dried out and open.

## San Francisco Insurance & HOA Coordination

A good share of the reconstruction work we handle in the city involves condo and co-op buildings in SoMa and the Mission, where an HOA or building association has its own approval process layered on top of the insurance claim. We document the loss and the rebuild scope in a format adjusters can work with, and when an HOA requires its own contractor review or common-area sign-off before work starts, we build that into the timeline rather than treating it as a surprise mid-project. For single-family homes in Bernal Heights or the Richmond, the coordination is simpler, but the documentation habit stays the same: photos, scope notes, and a rebuild plan the carrier can match against the estimate.

## Local Note

A fair number of the multi-unit wood-frame buildings we work in around the Mission and SoMa fall under San Francisco's mandatory soft-story retrofit program, which applies to older buildings with specific ground-floor framing. When a fire or water loss forces structural work in one of these buildings, we check whether the retrofit status affects what we're allowed to rebuild and how, since reframing a wall that's part of a required seismic upgrade is a different scope than a standard repair. It's a detail that's easy to miss if you're not used to working in the city's older multi-unit stock.
