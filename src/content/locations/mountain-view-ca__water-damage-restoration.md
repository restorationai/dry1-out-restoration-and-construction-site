## Why Mountain View Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Mountain View's housing stock dates to the mid-century tract-building boom of the 1950s and 60s, when slab-on-grade construction was standard and copper or galvanized plumbing was the norm. Those original lines are decades past their intended service life in many homes, and slab leaks are a recurring cause of calls across Santa Clara County. The city's flat topography and heavier clay soils also mean rainwater and leaking fixtures don't drain quickly, so water that would run off a sloped lot elsewhere tends to sit against foundations and wick into baseboards and subfloor. Add in the wave of condo and apartment construction driven by the area's tech-industry growth, with shared plumbing stacks and tightly packed units, and a single appliance failure or pipe break can affect multiple living spaces before anyone notices.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Mountain View

We start by identifying the water source and shutting it off, then move into extraction with truck-mount and portable pumps depending on how much standing water we're dealing with. On slab homes, moisture meters and thermal imaging tell us how far water has traveled under flooring, which matters because carpet pad and laminate over a concrete slab can trap moisture against the surface long after the top layer feels dry. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed and adjusted daily based on moisture readings rather than left running on a fixed schedule. For multi-unit buildings we coordinate access and drying equipment placement with property management so tenants in adjoining units aren't disrupted more than necessary.

## Local note

One detail that trips up a lot of Mountain View water losses: homes with slab foundations often show damp carpet or warped flooring near the outer walls before anyone sees a stain on the ceiling or baseboard, because the water migrates laterally under the slab before it ever surfaces. If a floor near an exterior wall feels soft or cool to the touch after a storm or appliance leak, that's worth a moisture check even if nothing looks visibly wet yet.
