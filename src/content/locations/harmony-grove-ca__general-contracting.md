## Why Renovation Projects in Harmony Grove Look Different

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process

Every project starts with an on-site walkthrough where we document existing conditions with photos, not just for our records but for your insurance carrier if the renovation is tied to a prior loss. From there we scope the work, pull the required permits through the county or applicable local jurisdiction, and sequence trades so that rough-in work (plumbing, electrical, framing repairs) is inspected before walls close back up. For kitchen and bathroom remodels specifically, we coordinate cabinetry, countertop, and fixture lead times early, since those are usually the items that stretch a project timeline if they're ordered late. If the remodel is a post-damage rebuild, our team that handled the mitigation work hands off directly to the construction crew, so there's no gap where a homeowner has to re-explain the scope to a new contractor.

## Insurance and Permitting Coordination

A good share of the renovation work we take on in Harmony Grove starts as a covered claim, whether that's fire damage requiring a kitchen rebuild or water damage that turned into a full bathroom remodel. We document the original damage, the approved scope, and the completed work in a format adjusters recognize, and we handle the back-and-forth on supplements when a carrier's initial estimate doesn't match what the structure actually needs. For properties in planned developments or under an HOA, we also confirm exterior material and color requirements before ordering, since HOA review can add weeks to a project if it's skipped early.

## Local note

One pattern we see often in this part of the county: properties with well or septic systems require extra coordination before a kitchen or bathroom remodel moves forward, since relocating a sink or adding a fixture can affect septic load calculations that a municipal-sewer property would never need to consider. We flag this during the initial walkthrough rather than after demo has started, which is usually when homeowners are surprised by it.
