## Why Temecula Properties See Mold Issues

Temecula's inland valley climate is hot and dry for most of the year, which actually works against homeowners in one specific way: it convinces people that mold isn't a local problem. It is, it's just concealed. Many homes in Paloma del Sol and Temeku Hills were built in the 1990s and early 2000s with stucco exteriors over wood sheathing, and a cracked weep screed or overspray from drip irrigation can push moisture behind that stucco for months before a stain ever shows indoors. Add in the valley's wide day-to-night temperature swings, common during Santa Ana wind events, and you get condensation forming inside attics and around dual-pane windows, especially in homes with AC units pushed hard through late summer. Older wood-frame construction near Old Town Temecula has its own pattern: original lath-and-plaster sections combined with newer additions create seams where moisture gets trapped differently than it does in a fully modern build.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Temecula

We start with a visual inspection and moisture mapping using meters at suspect areas, attic access points, exterior wall penetrations, under-sink cabinetry, and slab perimeters where plumbing runs are common in this valley's tract construction. Thermal imaging helps flag temperature differentials behind drywall without opening anything up first. From there we take air samples (typically one indoor sample per affected zone plus an outdoor control, since Temecula's dry air gives us a clean baseline to compare against) along with surface tape-lifts or swabs on visible growth. Samples go to an independent lab, and we walk the findings through with you rather than handing over a report full of spore counts and no explanation. Our team holds IICRC AMRT mold training, so the sampling strategy is built around standard protocol, not guesswork.
