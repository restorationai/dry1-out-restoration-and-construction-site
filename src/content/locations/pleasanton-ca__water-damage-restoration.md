## Why Pleasanton Properties See Water Damage Issues

Pleasanton sits in the East Bay's inland valley, which means long dry summers followed by wet winter storm systems that can push a lot of rain through gutters, downspouts, and grading systems in a short window. Homes built before modern plumbing codes often still run original galvanized supply lines or aging water heaters, and those are common failure points we see across Alameda County generally. Newer construction isn't immune either: slab leaks and appliance supply line failures (dishwashers, washing machines, refrigerator ice makers) show up in houses of any age, and a slow leak behind a wall or under a slab can go unnoticed for weeks before flooring or baseboards start to show it.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Pleasanton

The first thing our crew does is find the water, not just the water you can see. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to track how far water has traveled behind drywall, under cabinetry, and beneath flooring, because surface-dry doesn't mean structure-dry. Extraction comes next: standing water is pulled with truck-mounted or portable extractors depending on the volume and how far equipment has to reach inside the home. Once the bulk water is gone, we set air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the room's size and the materials involved, drying the structure in place per IICRC S500 drying standards rather than guessing at a timeline. We check moisture levels daily until the readings confirm the structure is actually dry, not just dry to the touch.
