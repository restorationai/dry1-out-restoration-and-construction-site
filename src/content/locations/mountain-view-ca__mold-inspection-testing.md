Mold inspection and testing in Mountain View often comes down to one question: is what you're smelling or seeing actually mold, and how far has it spread behind the drywall or under the flooring? Between the Bay Area's marine layer fog rolling in most mornings and the slab-on-grade construction common in the city's mid-century ranch homes, moisture finds its way into wall cavities here in ways that aren't always obvious from a quick look at a ceiling stain.

## Why Mountain View Properties See Mold Issues

Mountain View sits low and flat near the southern edge of San Francisco Bay, and that geography shapes how moisture behaves indoors. The marine layer keeps relative humidity elevated through spring and early summer, even on days that feel dry and sunny by afternoon. Add in the irrigation systems that keep mature landscaping green through the dry months, often running close to foundation walls, and you get a steady source of moisture pressing against slabs and stucco that weren't built with modern vapor barriers in mind. Homes from the 1950s and 60s building boom, common throughout the city, were built on concrete slabs with minimal under-floor clearance, so when moisture gets in, it tends to travel laterally through wall cavities rather than pooling under a raised subfloor the way it might in other housing stock.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Mountain View

We start with a visual inspection and moisture mapping, using meters to check baseboards, window sills, and any area where staining or a musty smell has been reported. Thermal imaging helps flag temperature differentials that often point to hidden moisture behind drywall or around plumbing penetrations, particularly useful in slab-foundation homes where you can't just pull back a subfloor panel to look. Depending on what we find, we collect air samples with spore trap cassettes in affected and unaffected rooms for comparison, and surface samples from visible growth when present. Samples go to a third-party lab for analysis, and we walk through the results with you so you understand what the spore counts and species identification actually mean for your property before deciding on next steps.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Mold Inspection

A proper mold assessment relies on more than a flashlight and a guess. We use calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers to track humidity differentials room to room, and borescopes to look inside wall cavities without cutting large openings. Air sampling follows protocols consistent with IICRC AMRT guidance, and lab analysis is handled independently so results aren't influenced by who's doing the remediation work, which matters if you need documentation for a landlord, buyer, or insurance claim.
