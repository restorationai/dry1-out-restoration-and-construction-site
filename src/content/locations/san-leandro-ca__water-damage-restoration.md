## Why San Leandro Properties See Water Damage Issues

San Leandro sits on the flatlands along the eastern shore of San Francisco Bay, and that geography shapes how water behaves once it gets inside a building. Soils across the East Bay flatlands tend to be clay-heavy, which means they drain slowly, and that slow drainage pushes groundwater and storm runoff toward low points around foundations and crawlspaces rather than away from them. A home that floods once in a wet winter is more likely to flood again if that drainage pattern isn't addressed.

Housing age plays a role too. A large share of San Leandro's single-family homes went up during the postwar boom of the 1940s and 1950s, and that generation of construction often still carries galvanized steel supply lines or original cast-iron drain stacks. Both corrode from the inside out over decades, and a pinhole leak inside a wall can run for days before it shows up as a stain on the ceiling below. Winter atmospheric river storms that roll through the Bay Area add another layer of risk, driving water through roof flashing, window seals, and grade-level entry points on homes that were never built with today's rainfall intensity in mind.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in San Leandro

The first step is always extraction, pulling standing water off floors and out of carpet pad with truck-mount or portable extraction equipment before it sits long enough to soak into subfloor material. From there we assess what's actually wet, not just what's visibly wet. In a slab-on-grade home, moisture meters and thermal imaging let us track water that has migrated under flooring or up into drywall without tearing out materials unnecessarily.

Structural drying follows, set up and monitored in line with the IICRC S500 water damage standard. Air movers and dehumidifiers get positioned based on how the specific building is losing moisture, not a one-size layout, because a 1950s ranch with plaster walls dries differently than a newer addition with modern drywall. We document moisture readings daily until materials reach dry standard, which matters both for the quality of the drying job and for the paper trail your insurance adjuster will want to see.
