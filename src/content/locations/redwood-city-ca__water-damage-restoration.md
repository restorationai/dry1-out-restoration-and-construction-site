## Why Redwood City Properties See Water Damage Issues

San Mateo County's Mediterranean climate means Redwood City goes months with almost no rain, then takes the brunt of winter atmospheric river storms in a handful of intense weeks. That swing stresses roofs, gutters, and window flashing that sat bone-dry all summer and suddenly have to shed heavy, sustained rainfall. A lot of the city's housing stock is post-war construction, slab-on-grade or raised foundation homes built before modern vapor barrier standards, and those older slabs and crawlspaces are more prone to moisture intrusion than newer builds. Add the Bay-adjacent water table in the lower-lying parts of town, and a slow weeping leak can saturate subfloor for weeks before anyone notices a soft spot.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Redwood City

We start with extraction, pulling standing water from carpet, pad, and hard flooring before it migrates further into subfloor and framing. From there our technicians map moisture with meters and thermal imaging, because in a slab-on-grade home the surface can read dry while the concrete underneath is still holding water. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: calibrated air movers and dehumidifiers placed to match the actual drying curve of the materials involved, not a one-size-fits-all setup. Given the humidity swings typical of the Bay Area, we monitor relative humidity daily rather than pulling equipment the moment carpets feel dry, since Redwood City's damp winter air can stall the final stage of drying if gear comes out too early.
