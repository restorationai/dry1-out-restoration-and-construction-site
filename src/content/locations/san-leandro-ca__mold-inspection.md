## Why San Leandro Homes See Mold Issues

San Leandro's housing stock runs older than a lot of the East Bay, with plenty of mid-century bungalows and post-war tract homes that were built with minimal vapor barriers and tight, low crawlspaces. Add in the fog and onshore humidity that settles over flatland neighborhoods near the shoreline, and you get slow-drying wall cavities after any plumbing leak or roof intrusion. Older cast iron and galvanized plumbing common in these homes also tends to weep slowly at joints long before a visible leak shows up, which gives mold a head start in places no one checks, like under a kitchen sink cabinet or behind a water heater closet.

## What a Free Visual Mold Inspection Covers

When mold is visible, or has already been sampled by an independent third-party tester, our inspector walks the affected area and documents what's actually there: location, extent, likely moisture source, and condition of surrounding materials. We're not in the business of mold testing. We keep testing and remediation separate for good reason, so if air sampling or lab analysis is what you need, we'll point you to an independent mold tester rather than run that work ourselves. Our inspection is about the visible problem in front of us and what it will take to remediate it correctly, not a lab report.

## San Leandro Insurance Considerations

Mold claims in the East Bay get scrutinized closely, and carriers usually want to see documentation of the source before they'll talk about covering remediation. A clear, dated visual inspection report showing where the mold is and what's feeding it (a slow roof leak, a failed shower pan, condensation on an undersized crawlspace vent) gives you something concrete to hand an adjuster, rather than a verbal description of a smell.
