## Why Escondido Properties See Renovation Challenges

A lot of what drives remodel scope here comes down to age and soil. Homes near the Old Escondido Historic District were built long before current seismic bracing and electrical code, so a bathroom remodel often uncovers knob-and-tube wiring or undersized joists that need addressing before the pretty finishes go in. Out toward San Pasqual Valley and the city's eastern edges, expansive clay soils shift with the wet-to-dry swing of San Diego County's seasons, which shows up as cracked slabs, sticking doors, or stucco fractures that get folded into an addition or kitchen project once the contractor opens the walls. Escondido's hot, dry summers also push homeowners toward remodels that address ventilation and insulation at the same time, since a 1960s HVAC layout rarely keeps up with August afternoons.

## Escondido Insurance & HOA Coordination

HOA-governed properties near Felicita and some of the newer developments around the 92029 corridor often require architectural approval for exterior changes, window swaps, or anything visible from the street, even when the work is replacing storm or fire damage rather than a discretionary upgrade. We build that review time into the schedule rather than treating it as a surprise delay. On the insurance side, a post-damage rebuild runs through the same carrier that handled the mitigation claim, and we document scope, materials, and code-upgrade costs (if the loss triggers current code requirements on an older home) so the adjuster has what they need without the homeowner chasing paperwork.
