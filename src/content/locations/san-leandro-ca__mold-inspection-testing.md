**Wondering why that musty smell keeps coming back in your San Leandro home?** Mold inspection and testing is the first step toward a straight answer, and it matters more in a city where bay-adjacent flatlands keep humidity lingering long after a leak has dried on the surface. A visual check alone can miss what's growing inside a wall cavity or under a slab, which is why testing matters as much as the walkthrough itself.

## Why San Leandro Properties See Mold Issues

Much of San Leandro's housing stock went up in the postwar boom of the 1940s and 1950s, and a lot of it sits on slab foundations close to the San Francisco Bay's tidal flats. That combination means moisture can wick up through concrete slowly and quietly, feeding mold behind baseboards and under flooring without an obvious active leak. Add in the Bay Area's mild, wet winters and the marine layer that rolls in through much of the year, and you get indoor humidity that stays elevated longer than it would in a drier inland climate. Homes near the estuary and flatter parts of the city tend to see this more than homes on higher ground, simply because drainage is slower and soil stays damp longer after storms.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in San Leandro

We start with a visual inspection of the areas you're concerned about, plus the spots that commonly hide problems in Bay Area construction: crawlspaces, slab perimeters, under-sink cabinetry, and attic ventilation points. From there we use moisture meters to check materials that look dry but may not be, since surface dryness and sub-surface moisture are often two different things in slab-on-grade construction. If conditions warrant it, we collect air samples for spore testing and, where appropriate, surface samples from suspect materials. Results get compared against an outdoor control sample, which gives a clearer read on whether spore counts indoors are actually elevated or just reflecting what's already in the outside air. We document everything in a written report you can use for your own records or to share with an insurance carrier or a property manager.

## Local note

One thing that catches San Leandro homeowners off guard: a slab that feels bone-dry to the touch can still be feeding moisture into baseboards and drywall from below, especially in homes built before vapor barriers under slabs were standard practice. We've found that pairing a moisture meter reading at the baseboard with one at mid-wall height tells a more complete story than either reading alone, particularly in the city's older, flatter neighborhoods where groundwater sits closer to the surface.
