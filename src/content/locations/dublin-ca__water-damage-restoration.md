## Why Dublin Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Dublin was built out during the tract-home boom of the 1980s through the early 2000s, which means a large share of the housing stock sits on slab-on-grade foundations with plumbing runs poured directly into or under the concrete. When those lines shift or corrode, the leak often starts underground and works its way up, which is very different from the attic or upper-floor leaks common in older multi-story construction. Alameda County's clay soil also expands and contracts with seasonal moisture swings, and that movement can stress slab joints and underground lines over time. Add in the Tri-Valley's pattern of hot, dry summers followed by heavier winter storm systems, and pipes, fittings, and irrigation connections take on real seasonal stress that eventually shows up as a leak, a slow stain, or a sudden flood.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Dublin

The first step on any call is finding where the water actually is, not just where it's visible, since slab construction can hide a leak's true path. We extract standing water, pull back flooring or baseboards where needed to check for trapped moisture, and set drying equipment based on moisture readings rather than guesswork. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, which means we track moisture daily and keep equipment running until materials, not just the surface, read dry. For homes with engineered hardwood or tile over slab, which is common in Dublin's newer builds, drying timelines can run longer than a quick surface read would suggest, so we re-check before closing out the job.

In Dublin homes built after 1980 on slab foundations, the main water lines often run beneath the concrete rather than through an accessible crawlspace.
