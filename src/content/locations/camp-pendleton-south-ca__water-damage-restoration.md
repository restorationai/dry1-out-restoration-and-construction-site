## Why Camp Pendleton South Properties See Water Damage Issues

Camp Pendleton South sits low and close to the Santa Margarita River watershed, and that geography matters more than most homeowners realize until a pipe fails. Soil here holds onto moisture longer after seasonal rains, which slows how quickly crawlspaces and slab perimeters actually dry out even after the visible water is gone. Add in the persistent coastal humidity that keeps indoor relative humidity elevated most of the year, and you get a pattern we see again and again: surface flooring reads dry on a moisture meter while the subfloor or framing underneath is still saturated. Homes in this area also tend to run older galvanized or copper supply lines depending on when the property was built, and those lines corrode faster in salt-air conditions than they would further inland.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Camp Pendleton South

We start with extraction, pulling standing water off floors and out of carpet pad before it migrates into wall cavities. From there we run moisture mapping with penetrating and non-penetrating meters across floors, baseboards, and framing, because in this climate a surface reading alone doesn't tell the whole story. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, and given the ambient humidity we're working against here, we typically set more dehumidification capacity per room than you'd need in a drier inland location to actually pull moisture out of the air instead of just redistributing it. We monitor daily until materials hit dry standard, document everything with photos and readings for the claim file, and only then close out the job.

## Local Note

Because a lot of properties in this part of the county sit on or near the river's historic floodplain, we don't trust a dry-looking subfloor on its own.
