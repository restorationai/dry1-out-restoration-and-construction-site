## Why Pleasanton Properties See Mold Issues

Pleasanton sits in the Tri-Valley, inland enough that summer heat routinely tops the East Bay coastal cities by ten degrees or more, which means air conditioners run hard and condensate lines clog more often than in milder climates. A clogged line backs up into a drain pan or drywall cavity near the air handler, and by the time a homeowner notices a ceiling stain, the moisture has often been there for weeks. Housing stock across the city ranges from slab-on-grade ranch homes built in the 1960s through 1980s to newer two-story construction, and both styles share a weak point: stucco exteriors with poor flashing at window and door penetrations let wind-driven winter rain track behind the wall plane, where it can sit against sheathing long enough to support growth before it ever reaches the interior paint surface. Alameda County's clay-heavy soils in the surrounding hills also expand and contract with the wet-dry cycle, which over time can open hairline cracks in slab foundations and allow ground moisture to wick upward into baseboards and flooring.

## Our Mold Inspection Process in Pleasanton

The inspection itself is a visual walkthrough: we look at the area where mold has already been spotted, or where a third-party test has already confirmed a problem, and assess the extent of what's visible, likely moisture sources nearby, and the condition of surrounding materials. What we don't do is collect air samples, swabs, or tape lifts, and we don't run a lab analysis. California property owners are generally better served when the company assessing mold for remediation is not the same company selling the test results, so if sampling is needed to confirm something that isn't visible, that work goes to an independent, certified mold tester, not to us. Once mold is visible or has already been verified by that third party, our IICRC-trained team can scope and quote remediation work separately. Keeping the inspection and any lab testing independent protects the homeowner's insurance claim and keeps the process defensible if a dispute comes up later.

## Local note

Across the Tri-Valley, one pattern shows up often enough to be worth knowing: homes with attic-mounted air handlers tend to develop condensate-related moisture problems at the drywall seam directly below the unit, not at the unit itself, because the leak travels along a joist before it ever stains the ceiling. If a Pleasanton homeowner sees a faint ring on a hallway ceiling nowhere near a bathroom or kitchen, the attic HVAC closet is usually the first place worth checking before assuming a roof leak.
