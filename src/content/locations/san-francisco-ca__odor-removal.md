Smoke that rolls in from a North Bay wildfire season doesn't always leave with the haze. It settles into the fog that blankets the Sunset and Richmond Districts most afternoons, and that moist marine air carries odor molecules deep into old plaster walls, wood lath, and single-pane window frames long after the smell outside has cleared. Odor removal and deodorization in San Francisco isn't just about masking a smell, it's about getting ahead of a climate that keeps re-depositing it.

## Why San Francisco Properties See Odor Issues

A lot of the city's housing stock works against quick odor resolution. Victorians and Edwardians in Noe Valley and Bernal Heights still have original horsehair plaster, fir subfloors, and wool carpet padding that soaked up decades of cooking smoke, pet accidents, or tobacco residue before anyone called a restoration crew. In SoMa, converted lofts and live-work units often share HVAC trunk lines, so a kitchen fire or a tenant's cigar habit on one floor can push odor through ductwork into neighboring units. And the fog-belt dampness common to the Sunset and Richmond means fabrics, insulation, and framing rarely dry out fully between storms, which lets musty and smoke odors bind to moisture instead of dissipating on their own.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in San Francisco

We start by identifying the actual odor source rather than treating the air alone, since masking a smell without removing what's producing it just means it comes back with the next fog bank. That means pulling baseboards, checking subfloor cavities, and inspecting attic insulation in older homes where odor-causing residue tends to hide. Once we've isolated the source, we clean and seal porous materials that can't be fully deodorized, then apply thermal fogging to replicate how smoke penetrated the structure in the first place, driving the deodorizing agent into the same cracks and fibers the original odor reached. For vacant properties, ozone treatment handles stubborn cases, while hydroxyl generators do the work in occupied homes or buildings with pets and plants, since hydroxyl technology doesn't require evacuation the way ozone does.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

Ozone and hydroxyl aren't interchangeable, and choosing wrong wastes a service call. Ozone is an aggressive oxidizer that works fast on heavy smoke or fire odor but requires the space to be sealed and unoccupied during treatment, including pets and houseplants removed. Hydroxyl generators produce a gentler reaction that's safe to run while people, animals, and plants remain in the home, which makes them the better fit for most occupied Pacific Heights and Noe Valley residences where a full vacate isn't practical. Thermal fogging pairs with either method for structural odors trapped in wall cavities, and we calibrate equipment runtime to the room's air exchange rate rather than running a fixed cycle on every job.
