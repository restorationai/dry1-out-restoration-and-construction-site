## Why South San Francisco Properties See Water Damage Issues

South San Francisco sits low against the bay, and a lot of the city, particularly the flatter areas near the industrial corridor and the older residential streets climbing toward Sign Hill, was built on fill and reclaimed marsh ground decades ago. That soil holds moisture close to the surface, which means crawlspaces and slab foundations often run damp even without an obvious leak. Add to that a housing stock that's heavily post-WWII construction, with cast iron or galvanized supply lines well past their service life, and you get a city where pipe failures, slow leaks behind walls, and appliance supply line bursts show up more often than in newer builds. The persistent coastal fog and limited sun exposure on north-facing lots also slow natural evaporation, so a wet wall cavity that might dry on its own in a sunnier inland city can sit saturated for days here, long enough for mold to start colonizing framing and drywall.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in South San Francisco

We start by identifying the source and extent of the water, using moisture meters and thermal imaging to map how far it's traveled behind walls, under flooring, and into subfloor cavities, not just where it's visible. Standing water comes out first with truck-mount or portable extraction equipment, followed by placement of air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the structure and the amount of moisture involved. Drying is tracked and documented against the IICRC S500 standard, with daily moisture readings so we know when materials have actually returned to a safe range rather than guessing. In South San Francisco's older homes, that often means paying closer attention to plaster and lath walls, which hold water longer than modern drywall and need extended drying time even after the surface feels dry. Any materials that can't be dried, like saturated insulation or delaminated subflooring, get removed and documented for the rebuild phase.
