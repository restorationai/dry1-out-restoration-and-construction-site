## Why Redwood City Properties See Mold Growth

Redwood City's housing stock runs the full range, from mid-century ranch homes with vented crawlspaces near the flatter, bay-adjacent sections of the city to newer slab-on-grade construction further inland. Crawlspace homes are prone to ground moisture intrusion, especially where grading has settled over the decades and water pools near the foundation instead of draining away. Slab homes have their own risk profile: plumbing runs inside the slab, and a slow leak can wick moisture into flooring and baseboards for weeks before it's visible. Add in the marine layer that rolls in off the bay on summer mornings and keeps indoor humidity elevated even when it's dry outside, and you get conditions where mold can establish in bathrooms, laundry rooms, and attic spaces without an obvious water event ever happening.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Redwood City

We start with a visual inspection of the areas you're concerned about, plus the spots that commonly get missed: attic sheathing, the underside of subfloors in crawlspace homes, and areas around HVAC returns where condensation collects. A moisture meter check on walls, framing, and flooring tells us whether we're dealing with a surface issue or something that's been developing behind the finish materials. Where visual and moisture readings suggest hidden growth, or where a property owner needs documentation for a sale, a lease dispute, or an insurance question, we collect air samples and surface samples and send them to an independent lab for analysis. You get lab-backed results, not a guess, and that report is what tells you whether remediation is actually needed and how extensive it should be.

## Local note

Older Redwood City homes built before modern vapor-barrier requirements often have crawlspaces with bare soil underneath instead of sealed plastic sheeting. Over time, ground moisture evaporates upward into the subfloor, and in homes where the crawlspace vents were painted over or blocked during a past remodel, that moisture has nowhere to go. It's one of the more common hidden-mold setups we flag during inspection, and it's easy to miss from inside the house because the subfloor itself usually looks fine until you check moisture content directly.
