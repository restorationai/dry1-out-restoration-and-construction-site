## Why San Francisco Properties See Mold Issues

A lot of San Francisco's housing stock predates modern vapor barriers and mechanical ventilation. Victorians and Edwardians in Noe Valley and Pacific Heights often have minimal attic airflow and crawlspaces that stay damp well into summer because the fog never fully burns off in those pockets of the city. In the Sunset and Richmond Districts, homes sit closer to the coastal fog belt itself, and we routinely find elevated moisture readings in exterior-wall insulation even when there's no obvious plumbing failure. Add in the stucco-over-wood-frame construction common in the Mission District and Bernal Heights, and you get a building envelope that can trap condensation behind an otherwise sound-looking wall for a long time before anyone notices a smell or a stain.

SoMa and other dense mixed-use corridors bring a different variable: converted live-work units and ground-floor residential spaces built near grade, where rising damp and poor slab ventilation can feed mold growth in baseboards and lower drywall sections. None of these patterns mean every home has a problem, but they do mean an inspection here benefits from someone who already expects to find moisture in places a drier climate wouldn't produce it.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in San Francisco

We start with a visual assessment of the areas you're concerned about, plus adjacent spaces that commonly hide problems in this climate: attic framing, crawlspace sill plates, and the wall cavities around older single-pane windows where condensation collects. From there we use moisture meters and, where useful, thermal imaging to find damp material that isn't visible yet. If conditions warrant it, we collect air samples to measure spore counts against an outdoor control sample, and surface or tape-lift samples from any suspect growth. Everything goes to an independent lab, and our AMRT-certified technician interprets the results in a written report that explains what was found, where, and what it means for next steps. We don't perform the inspection and then sell you the remediation decision in the same breath, the report stands on its own.

## Local note

In Sunset District and Richmond District homes built before the 1960s, we frequently see mold concentrated at the base of exterior walls facing the ocean side of the house, not because of a leak, but because that orientation catches the heaviest fog-driven moisture over the course of a year. When we test one of these properties, we make it a point to sample that ocean-facing wall separately from the rest of the house rather than relying on a single average reading, because the two can tell very different stories.
