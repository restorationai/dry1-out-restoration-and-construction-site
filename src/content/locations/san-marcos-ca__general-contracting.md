San Marcos is a city of contrasts: decades-old single-story homes ringing Lake San Marcos sit a few miles from the hillside subdivisions of San Elijo Hills, and both bring different renovation challenges. A kitchen remodel in a 1970s ranch house calls for different planning than a bathroom remodel in a 2005 hillside build on expansive soil. Dry 1 Out Restoration and Construction handles renovations, remodels and general contracting across San Marcos with that distinction built into how we scope and permit the work.

## Why San Marcos Properties Need a Tailored Renovation Approach

Housing stock here splits roughly into two eras. Near Lake San Marcos and Richland, you'll find older single-story homes with original plumbing runs, undersized electrical panels, and slab foundations that have shifted slightly over the decades. In San Elijo Hills and parts of Twin Oaks Valley, homes are newer but sit on graded hillside lots where expansive clay soils common across inland North County can cause slow foundation movement, stress stucco, and open hairline cracks around door and window frames. A kitchen remodel that reroutes plumbing or adds square footage has to account for which of these conditions applies before a single permit gets filed. We also see a fair number of homes near Cal State San Marcos and Palomar College converted into rental units, which brings its own code requirements for egress and separate utility metering when a remodel changes the floor plan.

## Our Renovations, Remodels and General Contracting Process in San Marcos

We start with an on-site walkthrough to confirm structural condition, existing permits on file with the City of San Marcos, and whether the lot falls under any hillside or drainage overlay. From there we draft a scope for kitchen remodels, bathroom remodels, or full post-damage rebuilds, pull the necessary permits, and sequence trades so inspections happen at the right stage rather than after the fact. For homes built before the 1990s, we budget extra time for plumbing and electrical discovery once walls open up, since what's on the original permit rarely matches what's actually behind the drywall. For hillside properties, we coordinate with a soils engineer when a remodel touches the foundation or adds load, which is common in San Elijo Hills additions.

## San Marcos HOA Coordination and Permitting

A large share of San Marcos's newer construction, particularly around San Elijo Hills and sections of Twin Oaks Valley, falls under active HOA architectural review. Exterior changes, roofline modifications, and even some window replacements during a remodel need committee approval before the city will issue a permit, and HOA turnaround times vary widely by community. We submit the architectural packet alongside the city permit application so both tracks move in parallel instead of stalling the project while one waits on the other. Older neighborhoods closer to Lake San Marcos generally don't carry HOA restrictions, which simplifies timelines but puts more weight on matching original architectural character for resale value.

## Post-Damage Rebuilds: Where Restoration Meets Renovation

Because Dry 1 Out also handles water and fire damage restoration under IICRC-certified protocols, we see a steady stream of projects that start as a mitigation job and turn into a full rebuild, a kitchen gutted by a slow leak under the sink, a bathroom rebuilt after a supply line failure, or a room reframed after smoke and fire damage. Rather than handing the rebuild to a separate contractor once the drying equipment comes out, our general contracting team takes the same property from demolition through finished construction, which keeps the insurance documentation and scope of work consistent from first notice of loss to final walkthrough.

## Local note

Properties downhill from Double Peak Park and along the ridgelines in San Elijo Hills often have graded retaining walls installed during original construction. When a remodel adds a room addition or extends a footprint near one of these walls, the city typically requires an engineer's letter confirming the wall's load capacity before framing permits are issued. We flag this during the initial walkthrough so it doesn't surface as a surprise once construction is already scheduled.
