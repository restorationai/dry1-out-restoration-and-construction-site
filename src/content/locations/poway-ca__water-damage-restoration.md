## Why Poway Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Poway's housing stock dates to the 1960s through 1980s ranch-style boom, built on concrete slab foundations rather than raised crawlspaces common elsewhere in the county. When a slab leak or supply line failure happens, water has nowhere to go but sideways under flooring and into interior walls, which means visible staining often shows up far from the actual source. Poway's clay-heavy soil also expands and contracts with the region's dry summers and wet winter storms, putting stress on buried pipe joints and slab control joints over time. In the city's more rural, equestrian-zoned pockets, properties on well water or septic systems add another layer, since a failed pressure tank or a saturated leach field behaves differently than a straightforward municipal plumbing failure and needs to be assessed before we start extraction.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Poway

We start with moisture mapping, not guesswork, using penetrating meters to find out how far water has traveled under slab flooring or inside wall cavities before we touch a single tool. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable units depending on how much square footage is affected. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, but we calibrate equipment differently for Poway's inland climate, where summer heat and low humidity can make surfaces feel dry to the touch while moisture is still trapped under baseboards or within double-layer drywall. We track every room with daily moisture readings until materials hit dry standard, not just until they look dry, and we document that data for your insurance file.
