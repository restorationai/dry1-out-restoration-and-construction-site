## Why Oakland Renovations Generate More Cleanup Than Expected

Much of Oakland's housing stock predates 1950, especially in Rockridge, Temescal, and the flatter blocks near Fruitvale. Older lath-and-plaster walls shed a chalky dust when cut into that settles into floor grain, baseboards, and HVAC returns far more aggressively than drywall dust does. Add in Oakland's dry summer winds carrying ash and pollen through open job sites, and a contractor's "broom clean" often leaves a fine film on every horizontal surface. Hillside homes in Montclair add another layer: construction debris tracked in on muddy boots during the wetter months settles into original hardwood and tile that wasn't sealed for heavy foot traffic. None of this shows up on a punch list, but it's exactly what a buyer, tenant, or homeowner notices first.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process

We start with a walkthrough to flag problem areas: window tracks packed with sawdust, light fixtures dulled by drywall mud, cabinet interiors never wiped after installation. From there the work moves top to bottom, ceiling fans and crown molding first so dust falls onto surfaces still to be cleaned, not ones already finished. We vacuum with HEPA-filtered equipment before wet-wiping, which matters in older Oakland homes where disturbed dust can include lead-based paint residue from pre-1978 construction. Floors get a final detail pass calibrated to the material: hardwood near Grand Lake gets a dry microfiber and pH-neutral solution, while tile and stone common in Jack London Square loft conversions can handle a more aggressive degreasing pass for adhesive overspray. We finish with glass, fixtures, and a final inspection sweep so nothing gets missed before keys change hands.
