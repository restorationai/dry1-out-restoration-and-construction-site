## Why San Jose Properties See Mold Inspection Issues

Santa Clara Valley's clay-heavy soil swells and contracts with the seasons, and that movement stresses slab foundations in newer Evergreen tract homes just as much as it does the pier-and-beam crawlspaces under older Willow Glen bungalows. Add a wet winter after a dry summer and you get hairline cracks that wick groundwater upward for months before staining shows on an interior wall. Homes near the Guadalupe River corridor and low-lying pockets close to Coyote Creek see a second pattern: humidity trapped under subfloors after storm season, which is exactly the kind of hidden moisture that surface inspection alone misses. Central air conditioning, common in post-1980s construction around Berryessa and Evergreen, also means ductwork can carry condensation-driven mold spores through a house long before a homeowner smells anything in the room where it started.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in San Jose

We start with a visual assessment and moisture mapping using a moisture meter and thermal imaging camera, checking the places San Jose homes actually fail: slab perimeters, bathroom exhaust terminations, and attic ventilation in homes with low-slope roofs common in the Rose Garden area. From there we collect air samples and, where surface growth is visible, tape-lift or swab samples, all handled under IICRC AMRT protocols and sent to an independent lab for analysis. Air sampling matters more in San Jose than it might in a drier climate inland, because the valley's marine-layer humidity swings mean a home can test clean on a dry August afternoon and show elevated spore counts after a wet February week. We give you lab results with plain-language findings, not just a spore count with no context for what it means for your home.

## San Jose Insurance & HOA Coordination

Many Almaden Valley and newer Evergreen developments sit under HOAs that require a documented third-party assessment before any remediation work begins, and some require a post-remediation clearance test from an independent hygienist before they'll sign off on a unit sale or refinance. We build our inspection reports to match what carriers and HOA management companies actually ask for: photos tied to moisture readings, lab chain-of-custody documentation, and a clear scope note on whether the finding points to a maintenance issue or a covered water loss. That distinction matters for San Jose claims, since many carriers treat long-term seepage differently than a sudden pipe failure.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Mold Inspection

Beyond moisture meters and thermal imaging, we use calibrated air pumps for spore trap sampling and, when ductwork is suspect, borescope cameras to check inside supply and return lines without cutting drywall unnecessarily. In crawlspace-heavy homes around Willow Glen and Cambrian Park, we also check subfloor ventilation and vapor barrier condition, since inadequate crawlspace venting is one of the more common root causes we find in homes built before 1960.

## Local Note

Homes built near the old Rose Garden and Japantown areas, many dating to the 1920s and 1930s, often have original redwood subflooring that can look structurally sound while holding moisture well above safe levels. Moisture meters calibrated for modern plywood sometimes under-read on old-growth redwood, so we cross-check with a pin meter and visual inspection rather than relying on a single tool, which matters if you're trying to catch a problem before it spreads past the original floor joists.
