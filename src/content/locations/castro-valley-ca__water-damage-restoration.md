## Why Castro Valley Properties See Water Damage Issues

Castro Valley sits in unincorporated Alameda County, which means permitting for any repair work tied to water damage, drywall, flooring, or framing goes through the county rather than a city building department, something we account for when scoping reconstruction. The terrain itself is part of the problem: much of the valley slopes toward San Lorenzo Creek and its tributaries, and homes built into the surrounding hills often have downhill-facing crawlspaces or daylight basements that collect runoff during the winter rainy season. Add in a housing stock that spans 1950s slab-on-grade ranches to split-level homes from the 1970s and 80s, and you get a wide range of plumbing ages and foundation types, each of which handles a leak differently. Galvanized supply lines common in the valley's older homes are also past their expected service life in many cases, and pinhole leaks inside wall cavities can run for days before anyone notices a soft spot in the drywall.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Castro Valley

We start every call with moisture mapping, using meters to find where water traveled, not just where it's visible, since gravity and wall cavities rarely cooperate with where the leak originated. Extraction comes next: truck-mounted or portable units pull standing water from flooring, carpet pad, and carpet before it wicks further into baseboards and cabinetry. From there we set structural drying equipment calibrated to the specific materials involved, plaster and older lath hold moisture longer than modern drywall, so a 1960s Castro Valley home may need a longer drying cycle than a newer build even with identical water volume. We track drying progress daily with moisture readings rather than guessing based on a calendar, and we document everything in a format insurance adjusters expect to see, since moisture logs and photos are what move a claim forward without delays.
