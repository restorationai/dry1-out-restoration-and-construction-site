In Old Escondido, houses built before 1950 often have original crawlspaces and single-pane windows that condense heavily on cooler nights, feeding mold behind baseboards and under flooring long before a homeowner notices a smell. ## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Escondido

We start with a visual assessment of the areas most likely to show growth, attics, crawlspaces, around windows, and anywhere there's been a known leak or water intrusion. From there we use moisture meters to check wall cavities and subfloor for elevated readings that aren't visible at the surface, since drywall and plaster can look dry while the material behind it is still saturated. If conditions warrant it, we collect air samples and surface swabs and send them to an independent lab for spore analysis, comparing indoor counts against outdoor baseline readings taken the same day. That comparison matters in Escondido specifically because ambient outdoor spore counts shift with the valley's agricultural activity, so a lab result needs a same-day outdoor control to mean anything.

Beyond moisture meters and lab-submitted air and surface samples, we use a borescope to look inside wall cavities without cutting large openings, which matters in Old Escondido's plaster-and-lath homes where unnecessary demolition can damage original finishes.
