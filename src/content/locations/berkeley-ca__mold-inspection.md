**Visible mold inspection in Berkeley?** If you're seeing dark spotting along a baseboard in a Claremont bungalow or a musty smell rising from a crawlspace in North Berkeley, a free visual mold inspection is the right first call, not a lab test. Berkeley's layered fog, hillside drainage, and pre-war building stock create the kind of hidden moisture pockets that let mold take hold quietly, often for weeks before anyone notices the smell.

## Why Berkeley Homes Show Visible Mold

A lot of Berkeley's housing stock predates 1950, and older framing, lath-and-plaster walls, and original foundation vents behave differently than anything built in the last few decades. In the flats near Southside and West Berkeley, older slab and raised-foundation homes sit close to grade, so irrigation runoff and the region's wet winters push moisture up through crawlspaces that were never built with vapor barriers. Up in the Berkeley Hills, homes cut into the slope often have one wall partially below grade, and hillside groundwater movement after a wet season can wick into framing long before it shows on drywall. Add the marine layer that rolls in most mornings, and north-facing rooms in neighborhoods like North Berkeley and Elmwood stay a few degrees cooler and a lot more humid than the rest of the house, which is exactly the condition mold needs to establish on grout lines, closet walls, and window sills.

## Our Free Visual Mold Inspection Process

When we walk a Berkeley property, we start where moisture problems actually originate: crawlspaces, attic ventilation points, under-sink cabinets, and any spot where a roof line, bay window, or flat-roofed addition creates a place for water to collect. We document what's visibly growing, note the staining pattern and material affected, and explain what that pattern usually tells us about the moisture source behind it, whether that's a roof flashing issue common on older Craftsman homes or a plumbing leak inside a wall cavity. This is a visual inspection, not a lab process. If the growth needs to be identified down to species or the air needs sampling, that work goes to an independent, third-party mold tester, never to us, because keeping testing and remediation separate is how the process stays honest for the homeowner.

## Independent Testing, Separate From Our Work

We don't run mold testing or air sampling, and we never will, even when a homeowner asks us to handle both ends. If you've already had a sample tested by an independent lab, bring us those results and we'll use them to scope the remediation. If nothing has been tested yet and the mold is clearly visible, our free inspection is often enough to move straight into remediation planning. Our team holds IICRC mold remediation training, which shapes how we assess what we're looking at, but the sampling and clearance work stays with a separate, independent party so there's no conflict between who tests and clears the space and who gets paid to fix it.

## Local note

A detail that catches a lot of homeowners off guard: flat-roofed mid-century additions near Southside and the blocks close to UC Berkeley's Sather Tower often have parapet walls that trap water at the roofline during heavy winter storms. That trapped moisture travels downward along the interior wall cavity, so the visible mold can show up a full story below where the actual leak is, inside a bedroom closet or above a bathroom vanity that has nothing to do with the plumbing in that room.

Berkeley's older homes also deserve a careful hand once any affected material needs to be opened up. Original lath, horsehair plaster, and early box-sill framing don't respond to moisture the way modern materials do, and we treat those materials with that in mind whenever an inspection leads into remediation work.
