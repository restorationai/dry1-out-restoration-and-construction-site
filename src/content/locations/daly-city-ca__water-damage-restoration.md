## Why Daly City Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Daly City's housing stock went up in the postwar building boom of the 1950s, often on graded hillside lots with crawlspaces or partial basements tucked below street level. That construction pattern means plumbing runs are older, slab penetrations and crawlspace vents are common entry points for water, and homes built into a slope tend to collect groundwater against the downhill foundation wall during the region's wet winter months. Add in the Pacific fog that rolls through most mornings, and you get a climate where materials rarely get a chance to fully dry on their own between rain events. A slow leak that would read as a minor nuisance in a drier climate can sit and saturate wall cavities for weeks in a home that never quite gets a dry stretch.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Daly City

We start with extraction, pulling standing water off flooring and out of carpet pad before it migrates further into subfloor or wicks up into baseboards and drywall. On hillside properties we pay close attention to crawlspace access points and downhill foundation walls, since that's typically where secondary moisture is hiding even after the visible water is gone. Moisture meters and thermal imaging tell us how far the water traveled behind plaster, tile, or paneling, which matters in older Daly City homes where original lath-and-plaster or early drywall assemblies hold moisture differently than newer materials. From there we set air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the room's size and material mix, and we track drying progress daily against the IICRC S500 standard until readings confirm the structure is back to a normal moisture range, not just surface-dry.
