## Why San Mateo Homes Are Prone to Hidden Moisture

Much of San Mateo's housing stock dates to the postwar boom of the 1940s and 1950s, with raised foundations and crawl spaces that trap humidity long after a rain event has passed. Add the Bay Area's persistent fog and onshore breeze, and you get indoor relative humidity that climbs even when there's no visible leak. Flat or low-slope roofs on mid-century ranch homes are another common culprit, since minor roofing failures can leak quietly into attic insulation for months before a stain appears on drywall below. Older plumbing in homes near the downtown core, galvanized supply lines in particular, is prone to pinhole leaks that saturate wall cavities slowly rather than all at once, which is exactly the pattern that lets mold establish before anyone notices a problem.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in San Mateo

An inspection starts with a visual walkthrough of the areas most likely to hold moisture: crawl spaces, attic sheathing, under-sink cabinets, and any spot where a past leak was reported. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to find elevated readings inside walls and subfloor that aren't visible to the eye, then follow with air sampling and surface swabs where conditions suggest active growth. Samples go to a third-party lab for species identification and spore-count analysis, and we walk the results through with you in plain language rather than handing over a report full of lab shorthand. Testing and sampling are performed consistent with IICRC S520 guidance, and our team holds the IICRC AMRT credential for mold-related work. If the results point to remediation, we can scope that separately, but the inspection itself is about documentation, not a sales pitch.
