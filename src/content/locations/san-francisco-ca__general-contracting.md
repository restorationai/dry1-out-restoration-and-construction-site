San Francisco's housing stock rarely cooperates with a straightforward renovation. A kitchen remodel in a Noe Valley Edwardian means working around original fir framing and knob-and-tube wiring that's still hiding behind newer drywall, while a bathroom remodel in a Sunset District stucco box means battling fog-belt moisture that's been working its way into the wall cavity for sixty years. Whether the job started with storm or water damage that needs a full rebuild, or it's simply time to update a tired kitchen or bath, general contracting here means building to current code inside a house that predates most of it.

## Why San Francisco Renovations Require Local Know-How

Most of the city's housing stock was built before 1950, and a lot of it before the 1906 earthquake and fire reshaped entire blocks. That means plaster-and-lath walls instead of drywall, original single-pane window openings, and foundations that were never engineered for the loads modern remodels put on them. In the Mission District and Bernal Heights, many homes sit on shallow perimeter foundations that were later retrofitted piecemeal, so a bathroom remodel that adds plumbing fixtures or a kitchen remodel that relocates a load-bearing wall often triggers a structural review before a single cabinet goes in. In the Sunset and Richmond Districts, persistent coastal fog keeps humidity higher than most homeowners realize, which is part of why post-damage rebuild work there has to account for materials that will see ongoing moisture exposure, not just a one-time leak.

We start with an in-person walkthrough to separate cosmetic wish-list items from anything structural, mechanical, or code-related, because in a pre-1950 home those categories blur fast. Kitchen and bathroom remodels typically involve more coordination than in newer housing stock, since relocating a sink or range often means updating adjacent electrical or venting that hasn't been touched since the house was built.

## San Francisco Permitting and Building Review

The Department of Building Inspection reviews most structural, electrical, and plumbing work, and anything touching the exterior of a home in a historic or design-review overlay, common in parts of Noe Valley and Pacific Heights, can pull in Planning Department review as well. Properties affected by the city's soft-story retrofit requirements sometimes need that seismic work addressed before or alongside a remodel if the renovation opens up framing near the ground floor. For condo and TIC owners in denser areas like SoMa, HOA or co-ownership agreements frequently require advance notice or board sign-off before a kitchen or bathroom remodel starts, particularly if plumbing lines run through shared walls. We build these review steps into the project timeline up front instead of treating them as a delay once the job is underway.

## Local note

Homes within a few blocks of Golden Gate Park and along the western edge of the Sunset District tend to run several degrees cooler and damper than the rest of the city on any given day, which is worth factoring into material choices for a remodel. Engineered wood and moisture-resistant backer board hold up better than solid hardwood or standard drywall in those microclimates, and we spec accordingly rather than defaulting to whatever's standard everywhere else in the Bay Area.
