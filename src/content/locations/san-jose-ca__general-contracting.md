## Why San Jose Homes Need a Different Renovation Approach

Santa Clara County's building stock spans nearly a century of construction methods, and that range shows up block by block. Willow Glen and the Rose Garden district still carry a lot of original lath-and-plaster and cast iron drain lines, which changes how a kitchen or bathroom remodel gets scoped, since you're often opening walls that weren't built to be opened twice. Almaden Valley and Cambrian Park, built mostly from the 1960s through the 1980s, bring their own quirks: aluminum branch wiring in some tracts, galvanized supply lines nearing the end of their service life, and slab foundations that have shifted slightly with decades of seasonal clay soil movement. Evergreen's newer construction is more forgiving structurally, but HOA architectural review adds a scheduling variable that older neighborhoods don't have. Any general contractor working across San Jose needs to read the house before writing the estimate.

We start with a walkthrough that looks past the finishes to the bones: wiring age, plumbing material, subfloor condition, and whether the work triggers a City of San Jose permit (most structural, electrical, and plumbing scope does). ## San Jose Permit and HOA Coordination

Structural and system-level changes, anything touching load-bearing walls, electrical panels, or main water lines, go through the City of San Jose's permitting process, and timelines vary depending on scope and current plan check volume. We submit what the project needs and schedule work around inspection windows rather than around our own crew calendar, which sometimes means a bathroom remodel pauses for a few days waiting on a rough-in inspection. In HOA communities like parts of Evergreen and newer Almaden Valley developments, exterior-visible changes (window replacements, siding, anything altering the street-facing elevation) typically need architectural committee approval before permits get pulled. We've learned to ask for that paperwork up front instead of mid-project, which is usually what keeps a remodel from stalling in week three.

## Local note

One detail that trips up out-of-area contractors: San Jose's Rose Garden and Japantown areas both sit on older municipal water infrastructure with a noticeable mineral content, which accelerates scale buildup in copper supply lines faster than in newer parts of the county. When we're re-piping during a kitchen or bathroom remodel in those neighborhoods, we check the existing line condition before assuming a partial repipe will hold, because scale buildup there often means the whole run needs replacing rather than just the fixture connection.
