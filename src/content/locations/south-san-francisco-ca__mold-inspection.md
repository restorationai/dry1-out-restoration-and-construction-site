## Why South San Francisco Homes See Mold Growth

Much of South San Francisco's housing stock went up in the postwar building boom of the late 1940s and 1950s, when the city grew quickly around its shipyards and industrial waterfront. Those homes often have shallow crawlspaces, minimal subfloor ventilation, and single-pane windows that trap condensation during the fog season. Add in the bay's humidity and the hillside drainage patterns common near San Bruno Mountain, and moisture tends to collect in the same low, shaded spots year after year: under bathroom vanities, behind refrigerators, along garage perimeter walls. Mold doesn't need a flood to take hold here. A slow condensation problem that nobody notices for a few months is enough.

## Our Free Mold Inspection Process

We keep this simple and we keep it honest about what it is. If mold is visible, or if an independent mold tester has already confirmed it, we come out and do a visual inspection at no cost. A technician walks the affected areas, checks for moisture intrusion points, moisture-meters the surrounding materials, and documents what's growing and where it's spreading. What we don't do is collect air samples, swabs, or tape lifts, and we don't run lab analysis. California property owners are often surprised to learn that testing and remediation are supposed to stay separate, and we agree with that approach: if you need spore counts or clearance testing, that work goes to an independent industrial hygienist, never to us. Once we know the scope from the visual inspection, we can talk through what a remediation plan would look like.

One thing we watch for in South San Francisco specifically: homes built into the hillside grade often have a daylight basement or partial crawlspace on the downhill side, and that's where fog-driven condensation collects fastest because the uphill soil stays saturated longer into the afternoon than the rest of the lot.
