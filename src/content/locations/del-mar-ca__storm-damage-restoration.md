## Why Del Mar Properties See Storm Damage

Del Mar sits on a narrow strip of coastal bluff and lowland between the Pacific and the San Dieguito River valley, which means storm exposure comes from two directions at once: wind-driven rain off the ocean and runoff draining down from the mesa above. Homes built on or near the bluffs often have stucco and wood-frame construction from several design eras, and salt-laden air accelerates corrosion on flashing, gutters, and fasteners long before a storm ever arrives. When an atmospheric river event stalls over San Diego County, that weakened envelope is where water finds its way in, through roof penetrations, window perimeters, and sliding door tracks that have expanded and contracted with coastal humidity for years.

Mature eucalyptus and torrey pines common to the area also shed large limbs in sustained wind, and root systems in sandy coastal soil don't always hold against saturated ground. A downed limb on a roof or fence is one of the more common storm calls we get from this part of the county, often paired with water intrusion where the impact cracked tile or lifted shingles.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Del Mar

The first step on any storm call is safety: checking for compromised roof structure, exposed electrical, and standing water near panels before anyone goes further than the entryway. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with moisture readings taken in framing, subfloor, and wall cavities, not just the surface, since coastal humidity keeps ambient moisture high and slows natural evaporation. Where tree limbs or wind have damaged roofing, siding, or fencing, our reconstruction crew handles the rebuild so you're not coordinating a separate contractor after the drying is done.
