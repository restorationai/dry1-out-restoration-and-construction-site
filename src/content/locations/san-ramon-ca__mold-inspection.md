**Mold Inspection in San Ramon?** San Ramon's warm, dry Diablo Valley summers give way to damp winter mornings when the valley fog settles in, and that swing between bone-dry and saturated air is exactly the kind of condition that lets a small leak turn into a visible mold patch before anyone notices. If you've spotted discoloration on drywall, a musty smell near a bathroom exhaust fan, or staining around a window frame, a free visual mold inspection is the right first call, not a lab test.

## Why San Ramon Homes See Mold Show Up

Much of San Ramon's housing stock went up during the valley's big build-out years, from the 1970s through the early 2000s, with stucco exteriors, slab foundations, and tile or composition roofs designed for a hot, dry climate rather than sustained moisture. Dual-pane windows and tight modern construction hold heat in well, but they also trap humidity when a bathroom fan is undersized or a kitchen exhaust vents into the attic instead of outside. Add in the occasional winter storm system that rolls through the Tri-Valley and overwhelms a gutter or flashing detail, and that trapped moisture has everywhere to go but into the drywall and insulation. We see the same pattern repeat at the inspection stage: a water stain that's been sitting behind a vanity or under a dishwasher long enough for mold to establish before the homeowner ever smells it.

## What a Free Mold Inspection Actually Covers

When mold is visible, or when an independent inspector has already tested and confirmed it, our technicians walk the affected area, document what's there with photos, and assess how far the moisture and growth have traveled behind the surface. We keep mold testing and mold remediation separate for good reason, so Dry 1 Out does not collect air samples, swabs, or tape lifts, and we never test our own future remediation work. If testing hasn't been done yet, we'll point you to an independent mold tester so the results stay unbiased before any cleanup decision gets made. Once visible mold or a third-party report confirms the scope, we can talk through what remediation would involve.

## HOA and Condo Considerations

A fair share of San Ramon's housing is HOA-governed, from attached townhomes to larger planned communities, and many associations have their own rules about who can access shared walls, crawlspaces, or exterior stucco for inspection purposes. If mold is suspected in a shared wall between units, or in a common-area utility chase, it's worth checking your HOA's governing documents before scheduling, since some associations require written notice or their own vendor approval. We're happy to work alongside property managers on timing, but the inspection itself still follows the same rule: visible mold or prior independent testing, and no sampling performed by us.

## Local note

One thing worth knowing about San Ramon specifically: homes close to the hillside grading in the newer subdivisions sometimes have landscaping or irrigation lines that slope moisture back toward the foundation instead of away from it, which shows up as mold along baseboards on exterior walls even when the roof and plumbing are fine. It's a landscaping and grading issue more than a house issue, but it's one of the more common causes we're asked to look at during a visual inspection in this area.
