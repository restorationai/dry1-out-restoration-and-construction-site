## Why San Mateo Properties See Fire Damage Spread Fast

Much of San Mateo's housing stock dates to the postwar boom, with stucco exteriors, tight-spaced bungalows, and multi-unit buildings built close enough together that a fire in one unit can push smoke into adjoining walls before anyone smells it. PG&E's public safety power shutoffs during dry, windy stretches also mean some homes run on backup generators or candles during fire-risk season, which adds a secondary ignition pattern we see regionally: small kitchen and electrical fires that start contained but smolder into wall cavities before they're caught. The coastal marine layer that rolls through San Mateo most mornings doesn't start fires, but it does affect how smoke odor settles. Damp, cool air holds soot particles closer to surfaces instead of letting them disperse, so homes here often smell worse the day after a fire than they did the night it happened.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process

We start with a damage assessment that separates what burned from what was affected by heat, soot, and the water or chemical agents used to put the fire out, since in most losses the smoke and suppression damage covers far more square footage than the fire itself. From there we board up any compromised openings, extract standing water left by firefighting efforts, and begin structural drying using the same moisture-mapping approach we'd use on a water loss, because wet, charred framing dries differently than wet clean lumber. Soot and smoke residue get cleaned from hard surfaces, and we run HEPA air scrubbers and thermal fogging equipment to break down odor at the molecular level rather than masking it. Work is documented and dried in line with IICRC S500 drying standards and our team's IICRC FSRT fire and smoke training, with photos and moisture logs kept for your insurance file.

## San Mateo Insurance and HOA Coordination

Fire claims in multi-unit buildings often involve both an individual homeowner's policy and a master HOA policy, and figuring out which one covers what, structure versus contents, shared wall versus unit interior, can slow a claim down if nobody's tracking it. We document damage by location and material so your adjuster (and the HOA's, if one is involved) gets a clear record instead of a guess. For standalone homes, we photograph and log everything from charred framing to smoke-stained drywall before any demolition starts, which keeps the claims process moving instead of stalling on "what did it look like before."
