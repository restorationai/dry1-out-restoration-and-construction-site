**Noticing a musty smell near a bathroom or crawlspace in your San Ramon home?** Mold inspection and testing is the first step to knowing whether that smell, water stain, or allergy flare-up is actually mold, and if so, how far it has spread before anyone starts tearing into drywall. San Ramon's mix of 1970s-80s tract construction and newer Tri-Valley developments each hide moisture differently, which is why a proper inspection matters before any remediation plan gets written.

## Why San Ramon Homes Develop Mold Issues

San Ramon sits in the Tri-Valley, where summer heat pulls moisture out of the air during the day but overnight temperature swings and irrigation runoff keep crawlspaces and slab perimeters damp longer than homeowners expect. Older tract homes built through the 1970s and 80s often have minimal crawlspace ventilation and original cast-iron or galvanized plumbing that seeps slowly at joints long before a visible leak shows up. Newer construction in the area tends to use tighter building envelopes for energy efficiency, which is good for utility bills but can trap humidity around windows, HVAC closets, and bathroom exhaust runs if a vent isn't ducted all the way outside. Clay-heavy soil common across the valley floor also expands and contracts with the seasons, which can stress foundation seals and slab penetrations enough to let groundwater intrude after a wet winter.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in San Ramon

An inspection starts with a visual walkthrough of moisture-prone areas: bathrooms, kitchens, laundry rooms, attic decking, and crawlspace framing. We use moisture meters and, where warranted, a thermal imaging camera to find damp framing or subfloor behind finished surfaces without opening walls unnecessarily. If visible growth or elevated moisture readings point to a problem, we collect air samples and, when appropriate, surface tape or swab samples for lab analysis to identify spore types and concentrations. Results get compared against an outdoor air sample taken on the same property, since a useful mold spore test always needs a local baseline rather than a generic number. The written report lays out what was found, where, and what remediation scope (if any) is actually justified, which keeps the next phase focused instead of guesswork.
