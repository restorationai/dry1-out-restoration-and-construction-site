## Why Mountain View Properties Show Mold Signs

Much of Mountain View's housing stock dates to the mid-century boom years of Silicon Valley's growth, when slab foundations, low-pitch roofs, and minimal attic ventilation were standard. Those slabs sit on Santa Clara Valley clay that expands and contracts with the wet winter and dry summer cycle, which over the decades opens hairline cracks that let groundwater wick upward into baseboards and drywall. Add in older aluminum-frame windows that condense heavily on foggy mornings, and bathrooms or laundry rooms without real exhaust ventilation, and you get the two classic starting points for mold: trapped moisture and a cool surface for it to collect on. Flat-roofed additions common on older Mountain View lots are another spot worth a look after any rainy stretch, since ponding water finds its way through roofing seams slowly, long before a stain shows up on the ceiling below.

## What Our Free Visual Mold Inspection Covers in Mountain View

Our inspection is a visual, no-cost walkthrough of the areas where you've seen staining, smelled must, or already had air or surface samples pulled by an independent tester. We look at the material itself, whether it's drywall, baseboard, subfloor, or cabinetry, check for the moisture source feeding it, and document what we find with photos so you have a clear record for a contractor, landlord, or insurance adjuster. What we don't do is collect samples or run lab testing ourselves. We keep mold testing and mold remediation separate for good reason, so if your situation needs air sampling or spore counts to confirm what's growing, we'll point you to an independent mold tester first. Once you have visible mold or a completed third-party test in hand, our team can inspect the affected areas and, if remediation is needed, scope that work separately.

## Local note

One thing worth knowing if you're in an older Mountain View condo or apartment complex: many of these buildings share plumbing chases and HVAC returns between units, so a leak or condensation problem in one unit can show up as a mold patch two doors down with no obvious water source on that side of the wall. If you're renting or in an HOA, it's worth asking the property manager or board whether any inspection or testing has already been done building-wide before you schedule your own, since that record often speeds up the process.
