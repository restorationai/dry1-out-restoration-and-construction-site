## Why Encinitas Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Encinitas doesn't get hurricanes or tornadoes in the way other regions do, but the atmospheric river storms that roll in off the Pacific every few winters bring sustained wind and heavy rain that older coastal construction wasn't designed to shed quickly. Homes in Old Encinitas and parts of Leucadia sit close enough to the bluffs that wind-driven rain gets forced horizontally under eaves and around window flashing that's already fatigued from decades of salt air exposure. Further inland in Olivenhain, larger lots with mature eucalyptus and oak cover mean storm damage more often shows up as a split limb through a roofline or a fence flattened across a driveway than as water intrusion. ## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Encinitas

The first visit is about stopping further loss, not starting the full repair. That means temporary tarping or board-up on any breach, extraction of standing water, and a moisture map of the affected framing and drywall before anything gets reopened. Salt-air corrosion changes the calculus on a lot of Encinitas jobs: fasteners and flashing that look intact can be more compromised than they appear, so our team checks beyond the visible wet line rather than stopping at the drywall's water mark. Structural drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines, with equipment placement adjusted for coastal humidity, since ambient moisture here doesn't drop off the way it does inland, which can stretch drying timelines if we don't compensate with stronger air movement and dehumidification from the start.

Most homeowner policies treat wind and storm-driven water intrusion differently than flood from rising water, which matters because a lot of Encinitas storm claims involve both in the same event, a tree down on the roof and water that got in through the breach before it was tarped. We document damage with photos and moisture readings as we go so the distinction is clear for the adjuster.
