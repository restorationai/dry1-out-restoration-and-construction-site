## Why South San Francisco Properties See Mold Issues

South San Francisco sits in a stretch of the Peninsula where coastal fog pushes through the hills most of the year, keeping outdoor humidity higher for longer stretches than cities further inland. Older homes here, many built in the postwar decades before continuous vapor barriers and modern attic ventilation were standard, were not built with that moisture load in mind. Add in a housing stock where older plumbing shares tight crawlspaces and bathroom walls with minimal ventilation, and a slow leak can sit undetected for weeks while mold colonizes the backside of drywall or subfloor. Low-lying parcels near the bay shoreline, like much of the Bay Area's fill-built flatlands, also drain slower after heavy rain, which keeps foundation-adjacent soil damp longer and pushes moisture up through slab edges and sill plates.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in South San Francisco

An inspection starts with a visual walkthrough, checking the places mold actually hides: behind baseboards, under sinks, inside HVAC returns, and along the roofline where fog-driven condensation collects on the underside of sheathing. From there, our AMRT-trained technicians use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map where materials are reading wet even if the surface looks dry, since a stain can be old while the framing behind it is still actively damp. Depending on what the walkthrough turns up, we collect air samples with a spore trap, surface samples with tape lift or swab, or both, and send them to an independent lab for analysis rather than reading results ourselves. You get a written report with lab findings and a plain-language explanation of what was found and where, which is the document most insurance carriers and property managers want before any remediation work is approved.

## Local note

The afternoon fog that rolls through South San Francisco's gap in the hills doesn't just cool things down, it keeps relative humidity elevated well into the evening, often longer than it stays elevated in inland Bay Area cities on the same day. When we schedule air sampling, we try to get it done before that fog settles in and skews ambient moisture readings, because testing mid-afternoon on a foggy day can read differently than testing the same space at 9am.
