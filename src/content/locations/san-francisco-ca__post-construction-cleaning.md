San Francisco's renovation boom from Noe Valley's Victorians to SoMa's live-work lofts leaves behind a layer of fine dust, adhesive residue, and drywall grit that standard janitorial crews aren't equipped to handle. Post-construction and specialty cleaning closes out a remodel or new build properly, pulling debris and particulate out of corners, vents, and finishes before a final walkthrough or occupancy inspection. In a city where fog and humidity swing daily, timing that final clean matters as much as the method.

## Why San Francisco Properties See Post-Construction Cleaning Issues

Much of the city's housing stock predates 1950, with ornate trim, built-in cabinetry, and plaster details in neighborhoods like Pacific Heights and the Richmond District that trap drywall dust in ways flat modern surfaces don't. Add in the Sunset and Richmond's fog-belt moisture, and fine construction dust that would stay loose in a drier climate instead clings to window tracks, baseboards, and HVAC returns almost immediately. Contractors working near Golden Gate Park and the avenues often find that a job cleaned on a dry afternoon still shows a dusty film by the next morning if the final pass skipped a damp-wipe step. Row houses in the Mission District and Bernal Heights, many built close together with shared walls, also mean debris staging and dust containment have to be tighter to avoid drift into neighboring units.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process

We start with a bulk debris pull: leftover materials, packaging, and larger offcuts that general contractors leave behind. From there, HEPA-filtered vacuuming handles fine particulate on floors, sills, and ledges before we move to detail work on light fixtures, cabinet interiors, and HVAC vents, where drywall dust tends to settle last. Glass and mirror surfaces get a separate pass since construction film doesn't respond the same way to standard glass cleaner, and we finish with floor-specific treatment, whether that's a damp mop on tile or a dry microfiber pass on engineered hardwood common in newer SoMa and Mission builds. On jobs near 94103 and 94110 we've noticed adhesive residue from tile and flooring work needs a solvent-safe approach that won't haze surrounding finishes.

## San Francisco HOA Coordination

Condo and co-op buildings in SoMa and Pacific Heights often require debris to move through freight elevators on a reserved schedule, with protective floor coverings in common hallways during the cleanout. We coordinate directly with building management or the HOA to book that elevator window and confirm what materials can leave through the lobby versus a service entrance, so a final clean doesn't stall because debris removal missed a building's access rules.

## Local note

In the Sunset and Richmond Districts, fog rolling in overnight can re-humidify a space that looked clean the evening before, turning loose drywall dust into a thin paste on sills and counters by morning. We schedule final detail passes for late afternoon when humidity has dropped, and we recommend owners hold off on photos or walkthroughs until that window to avoid a callback for a surface that re-dusted itself.
